## Report

The three pie charts illustrate how an individual school in the United Kingdom distributed its annual financial budget across five distinct expenditure categories in 1981, 1991, and 2001.

Overall, teachers' salaries consistently constituted the largest share of the school's total budget throughout the two-decade period, whereas insurance consistently accounted for the smallest proportion. In addition, spending on furniture and equipment and educational resources exhibited considerable volatility, whereas expenditure on other staff salaries experienced a sustained downward trend.

In 1981, teachers' salaries represented 40% of overall expenditure, followed by other workers' salaries at 28%. Spending on educational resources and furniture and equipment each comprised 15%, while insurance took up a minimal 2%. By 1991, teachers' salaries peaked at 50%, and resources rose to 20%. In contrast, outlays on other workers' salaries and furniture/equipment contracted to 22% and 5% respectively, while insurance edged up to 3%.

By 2001, the proportion devoted to teachers' salaries moderated to 45%, remaining the dominant category. Meanwhile, furniture and equipment surged to 23%, and insurance reached a period high of 8%. Conversely, other staff salaries and educational resources dropped to 15% and 9% respectively.
