## Report

The bar graph compares the proportions of waste treated using four different methods—burning, chemical treatment, recycling, and landfilling—across four countries: the Netherlands, the UK, Italy, and Spain.

Overall, recycling is the most widely used method in the Netherlands, whereas landfilling is the dominant approach in the UK. Spain records the highest percentage of waste being burned, while chemical treatment accounts for a fairly similar proportion in all four countries.

Looking first at recycling, the Netherlands has the highest figure, with nearly 70% of its waste being recycled. The UK follows closely at around 65%, while Italy recycles a smaller proportion. Spain has the lowest recycling rate among the four countries. Conversely, burning is most common in Spain, where approximately 30% of waste is disposed of in this way. Italy ranks second at about 22%, whereas the UK burns only around 4% of its waste, the lowest amount in the chart.

As for chemical treatment, the percentages are relatively consistent across all four countries, remaining close to 17%. Italy and Spain record slightly higher figures than the UK, while the Netherlands has the lowest proportion. Finally, landfilling is most prevalent in the UK, accounting for roughly 65% of waste disposal. Italy and Spain both landfill around 40% of their waste, whereas the Netherlands relies on this method the least, at only about 8%.
