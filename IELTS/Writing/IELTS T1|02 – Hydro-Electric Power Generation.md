## Report

The provided diagram illustrates how electricity is produced by a hydro-electric power station. Overall, the process is cyclical. Water is collected in a reservoir after rainfall, used to generate electricity, and then pumped back to the reservoir so that the cycle can continue.

First, heat from the sun causes seawater to evaporate. The water vapor forms clouds and later falls as rain, which fills a reservoir behind a dam. When a valve is opened, water flows through a pipe to a turbine. As the water passes through, it spins the turbine, which produces electricity.

Next, electricity is sent to a transformer station via high-voltage cables, where the voltage is increased for efficient transmission. It is then carried by underground cables to public buildings such as hospitals and schools.

After leaving the turbine, the water is collected at a lower level. A pump then returns it to the reservoir, allowing the same water to be reused for generating electricity whenever needed.
