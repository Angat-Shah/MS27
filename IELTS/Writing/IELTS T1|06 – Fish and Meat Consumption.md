## Report

The line graph compares the consumption of four different types of meat and fish in a European country over a period of 25 years, from 1979 to 2004.

Overall, chicken consumption showed a strong upward trend and became the most popular option by the end of the period. In contrast, both beef and lamb followed a downward trend, whereas fish consumption remained relatively stable despite a slight decline.

In 1979, beef was the most widely consumed meat at around 220 grams per person per week, followed by lamb at approximately 150 grams. Beef consumption rose slightly to a peak of about 240 grams in the early 1980s before declining sharply to around 110 grams by 2004. Similarly, lamb intake decreased steadily throughout the period, ending at just over 70 grams.

Chicken consumption, on the other hand, increased continuously from about 140 grams to approximately 250 grams per person per week, overtaking all other options by 2004. Fish remained the least consumed category throughout the period, without any remarkable change in consumption.
