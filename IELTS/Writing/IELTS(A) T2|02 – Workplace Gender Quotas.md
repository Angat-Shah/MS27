# IELTS T2|02 – Workplace Gender Quotas

**“In many countries, companies hire more men than women for certain positions. Some people believe that employers should be required to recruit equal numbers of men and women in every field. To what extent do you agree or disagree?”**

Word Count: **298**

## Essay

In many contemporary job markets, a noticeable gender imbalance persists across various professional sectors. Consequently, some argue that governments should mandate a strict fifty-fifty recruitment quota for men and women across all industries. While I strongly advocate for gender equality and non-discriminatory workplace practices, I largely disagree with enforcing mandatory numerical parity.

The primary reason against mandatory gender quotas is that recruitment should fundamentally prioritize competence, professional qualifications, and merit over demographic characteristics. Organizations thrive when they appoint the most qualified candidates to critical roles. If employers are legally compelled to maintain equal gender representation, they may be forced to pass over exceptionally skilled applicants simply to fulfill arbitrary numerical targets. Such policies could undermine meritocracy and inadvertently compromise workplace productivity and morale.

Furthermore, applicant demographics vary significantly across different career paths due to divergent educational backgrounds and personal preferences. For instance, technical and heavy manual industries, such as civil engineering and construction, historically attract a significantly larger pool of male applicants, whereas sectors like primary education and nursing often receive a higher proportion of female candidates. Mandating equal hiring numbers under such conditions is logistically unfeasible and places an unrealistic burden on businesses attempting to recruit from disproportionate applicant pools.

Nevertheless, eliminating systemic discrimination remains imperative. Rather than enforcing rigid quotas, governments and corporate entities should focus on providing equal opportunities, transparent selection procedures, and anti-bias training for hiring managers. Establishing supportive parental leave policies and encouraging female participation in STEM fields address the root causes of disparity more constructively.

In conclusion, although equal opportunity in the workplace is indispensable, legally compelling companies to hire equal numbers of men and women across every discipline is an impractical oversimplification. Recruitment should remain grounded in merit and aptitude within a transparent, equitable framework.
