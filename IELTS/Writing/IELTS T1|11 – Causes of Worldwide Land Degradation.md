## Report

The pie chart illustrates the primary causes of global agricultural land degradation, while the accompanying table delineates how these factors impacted three distinct regions—North America, Europe, and Oceania—during the 1990s.

Overall, over-grazing represented the single largest contributor to worldwide land degradation, closely followed by deforestation and over-cultivation. Among the regions, Europe suffered the highest overall proportion of degraded agricultural land, whereas North America recorded the lowest.

Globally, over-grazing was responsible for over a third of all land degradation, at 35%. Deforestation and over-cultivation accounted for 30% and 28% respectively, while miscellaneous factors comprised the remaining 7%.

Regionally, Europe exhibited the highest overall degradation rate at 23%, driven predominantly by deforestation (9.8%) and over-cultivation (7.7%), alongside over-grazing (5.5%). In stark contrast, North America experienced total degradation of just 5%, with over-cultivation being the primary factor at 3.3%, while deforestation contributed a negligible 0.2%. Meanwhile, Oceania registered an overall degradation figure of 13%, the vast majority of which was caused by over-grazing (11.3%), with over-cultivation having no recorded impact whatsoever (0.0%).
