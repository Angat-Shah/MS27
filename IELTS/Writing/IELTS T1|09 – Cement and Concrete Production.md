## Report

The diagrams illustrate the process of cement production and the subsequent use of cement in concrete manufacturing.

Overall, cement production involves several distinct stages, beginning with the crushing of limestone and clay and ending with the packaging of the finished product. By contrast, concrete is produced through a single mixing process involving four materials in fixed proportions.

Initially, limestone and clay are crushed together to form a fine powder. This powder is then thoroughly mixed before being fed into a rotating heater, where it is exposed to intense, continuous heat. Following this heating stage, the resulting material is ground into a fine cement powder, which is subsequently packaged in bags for distribution.

Concrete production is considerably simpler, as it requires only the combination of four ingredients in a concrete mixer. Gravel constitutes the largest proportion, at 50%, followed by sand at 25%, cement at 15%, and water at 10%. These ingredients are mixed thoroughly as the machine rotates, producing the final concrete.
