# IELTS Writing Master Guide -- Band 7+ Execution Framework

**Purpose:** A single, self-contained reference document for producing Band 7-8+ essays under timed conditions. Every template below is designed as a fill-in scaffold: analyse the question, identify the type, slot your content into the fixed structure, and write.

**Design Principles:**
- 4-paragraph structure for all Task 1 responses (Introduction, Overview, Body 1, Body 2).
- 4-paragraph structure as default for Task 2 (Introduction, Body 1, Body 2, Conclusion), with a justified 5-paragraph option documented where applicable.
- Word targets: Task 1 = 155-185 words. Task 2 = 270-300 words.
- Every template uses fixed linker positions. You do not need to decide which linker to use mid-exam; the positions are pre-assigned.
- Vocabulary is curated for natural, high-scoring usage. Avoid overusing any single phrase across both tasks.

# PART A: TASK 1 -- ACADEMIC REPORT (20 minutes, 150+ words)

---

## A.1 Universal Rules (Apply to Every Task 1 Response)

**Structure: Exactly 4 paragraphs. No exceptions.**

| Paragraph     | Function                                      | Sentences | Key Rule                                                    |
|:--------------|:----------------------------------------------|:---------:|:------------------------------------------------------------|
| Introduction  | Paraphrase the question statement              | 1         | Never copy the original wording verbatim. Change the verb, restructure the sentence, and replace nouns with synonyms. |
| Overview      | State 2-3 dominant patterns or features        | 2-3       | No numbers, no data, no specific figures. General trends only. Start with "Overall,". This paragraph single-handedly determines whether you score above 5 on Task Achievement. |
| Body 1        | Detail the first grouping with specific data   | 3-4       | Always include at least 2 data points with figures. Compare and contrast within the group. |
| Body 2        | Detail the second grouping with specific data  | 3-4       | Same as Body 1. End with a comparison or a closing data point. |

**Tense Rules:**
- Past dates (e.g., 1990-2010) = Past tense throughout Body paragraphs. Overview can use present tense for general observations.
- Future projections (e.g., 2025-2050, or "estimated/projected") = Future tense ("is expected to," "is projected to").
- No dates given (common with processes, some maps) = Present tense or passive present.
- Mixed past and future = Use past for historical data, future for projections, clearly separated.

**Grouping Strategy for Body Paragraphs:**
- Data charts: Group by similarity of trend (e.g., categories that rose together vs. categories that fell).
- Maps: Group by time period (Before vs. After) or by area (North vs. South).
- Processes: Group by phase (first half of stages vs. second half).

---

## A.2 Task 1 Templates by Category

Each template below follows the exact same 4-paragraph architecture. The only variation is the vocabulary set and the nature of what you describe.

### CATEGORY 1: DATA CHARTS (Line Graph, Bar Chart, Pie Chart, Table)

These four sub-types share one unified template because the task is identical: describe numerical data, identify trends, and make comparisons. The only difference is whether the data shows change over time (line/bar with time axis) or a static comparison (pie/bar without time axis, table).

**When to use:** Any question that presents numerical data in a visual or tabular format.

---

#### TEMPLATE 1A: DATA WITH A TIME AXIS (Line Graph, Bar Chart over time)

**Introduction:**
> The [line graph / bar chart] [illustrates / compares / presents] [what is measured] in/across [number] [categories / countries / groups] over a [number]-year period, from [start year] to [end year].

**Overview:**
> Overall, [category A] experienced the most [pronounced increase / significant decline], [rising from / falling to] the [highest / lowest] level by the end of the period. In contrast, [category B] [remained relatively stable / showed the opposite trend / underwent a more moderate change].

**Body 1:**
> In [start year], [category A] stood at [figure], making it the [highest / lowest / second-highest] among all categories. Over the following [number] years, it [rose steadily / climbed sharply / declined gradually] to reach [figure] by [mid-point year]. [Category B], which began at a [comparable / significantly lower] level of [figure], followed a [similar / contrasting] trajectory, [increasing / decreasing] to approximately [figure] during the same period.

**Body 2:**
> Turning to the remaining categories, [category C] [fluctuated between / hovered around] [figure] and [figure] throughout the period, showing no [discernible / clear] long-term trend. Meanwhile, [category D] [registered a marginal increase / experienced a steady decline] from [figure] in [year] to [figure] in [end year], ultimately [overtaking / falling behind] [category C] by [end year].

---

#### TEMPLATE 1B: DATA WITHOUT A TIME AXIS (Static Bar Chart, Pie Chart, Table)

**Introduction:**
> The [pie chart / bar chart / table] [provides a breakdown of / compares] [what is measured] across [number] [categories / countries / age groups] [in year / during the period of].

**Overview:**
> Overall, [category A] accounted for the [largest proportion / highest figure], while [category B] [represented the smallest share / recorded the lowest value]. [Optional third observation: There was a [notable / striking] disparity between [group X] and [group Y].]

**Body 1:**
> [Category A] constituted [the largest share at / the highest value of] [figure / percentage], [more than double / nearly triple] that of [category B], which stood at [figure]. [Category C] occupied the [second / third] position with [figure], followed closely by [category D] at [figure].

**Body 2:**
> By contrast, the remaining categories [collectively accounted for only / each represented less than] [figure / percentage] of the total. [Category E] and [category F] were [nearly identical at / recorded comparable figures of] [figure] and [figure] respectively. Notably, [category G] registered [the lowest figure / a negligible proportion] of [just / a mere] [figure], [underscoring the dominance of / highlighting the gap with] the leading category.

---

#### TEMPLATE 1C: COMBINED OR MULTIPLE CHARTS

Some questions present two charts together (e.g., a bar chart and a pie chart, or two tables). The structure remains 4 paragraphs. Dedicate Body 1 to Chart A and Body 2 to Chart B. Alternatively, if the charts share categories, group by category across both charts.

**Introduction:**
> The [first chart type] [compares / illustrates] [topic of chart 1], while the [second chart type] [provides information on / breaks down] [topic of chart 2]. Both relate to [shared topic / year / location].

**Overview:**
> Overall, [key finding from chart 1]. Additionally, [key finding from chart 2]. [Optional: A clear [correlation / contrast] can be observed between the two datasets.]

**Body 1:** Describe Chart 1 with data.

**Body 2:** Describe Chart 2 with data. Where possible, draw a connection to Chart 1.

---

### CATEGORY 2: MAP

**When to use:** Any question that shows a geographical area at two (or more) different points in time, or a proposed plan for development.

**Key Principles:**
- Use passive voice extensively ("A car park was constructed," not "They built a car park").
- Describe changes systematically by area (north to south, east to west) or by theme (infrastructure, green spaces, residential).
- For "before and after" maps, Body 1 = description of the original layout or the first time period. Body 2 = description of the changes.

---

#### MAP TEMPLATE

**Introduction:**
> The two maps [compare / illustrate] the [layout / development / changes] of [place name] [between year A and year B / before and after the proposed redevelopment].

**Overview:**
> Overall, [place name] underwent [significant / substantial / radical] transformation over the period, with the most notable change being [the replacement of X with Y / the expansion of residential areas / the addition of commercial facilities]. [The eastern/western/northern section experienced the most pronounced development, while the southern area remained largely unchanged.]

**Body 1 (Original State or First Time Period):**
> In [year A / the original layout], [place name] was characterised by [description: e.g., open farmland, a small cluster of houses, a single road]. [Feature A] was situated [to the north of / adjacent to / along the eastern boundary of] [landmark]. [Feature B] occupied the [centre / southern portion], and [Feature C] [ran parallel to / extended from X to Y].

**Body 2 (Changed State or Second Time Period):**
> By [year B / in the proposed plan], [Feature A] had been [demolished / replaced by / converted into] [new feature]. [A new facility, e.g., a shopping centre] was [erected / constructed / established] [in the immediate vicinity of / to the south of] [landmark]. Furthermore, [Feature B] was [extended / relocated / expanded to accommodate] [description]. [The road network was also reconfigured, with a new dual carriageway replacing the original single-lane road.]

---

**Change Verbs (Passive):**
was demolished, was knocked down, was converted into, was replaced by, was relocated to, was extended, was expanded, was constructed, was erected, was established, was built, made way for, was redeveloped, was upgraded, was renovated, was reconfigured, was widened, was removed, was cleared, was transformed into

**Directional and Positional Language:**
to the north/south/east/west of, in the north-eastern/south-western corner, adjacent to, in the immediate vicinity of, on the outskirts of, in the centre of, along the [road/river/coast], at the intersection of X and Y, running parallel to, extending from X to Y, situated between X and Y, on the opposite side of, bordering, overlooking

**Describing the Nature of Change:**
underwent significant redevelopment, was subject to extensive modification, remained [largely / entirely] unchanged, retained its original character, saw the addition of, witnessed the removal of, experienced considerable expansion, was noticeably altered

### CATEGORY 3: PROCESS DIAGRAM / FLOW CHART

**When to use:** Any question that shows stages, steps, or a cyclical/linear process (natural process, manufacturing, life cycle, flow chart).

**Key Principles:**
- Use passive voice as the primary voice ("The raw material is extracted," not "Workers extract the raw material").
- Use sequencing linkers to show the order of stages.
- Count the stages and mention the total in the Overview.
- Identify whether the process is linear (start to finish) or cyclical (repeats).
- Do not add opinion or evaluation. Simply describe.

---

#### PROCESS TEMPLATE

**Introduction:**
> The diagram [illustrates / depicts / outlines] the process by which [end product is produced / natural phenomenon occurs / X is transformed into Y].

**Overview:**
> Overall, [the process / this procedure] comprises [number] distinct stages, commencing with [the first step, e.g., the harvesting of raw materials] and culminating in [the final step, e.g., the distribution of the finished product to retailers]. [If cyclical: It is a cyclical process in which the final stage feeds back into the initial phase.]

**Body 1 (First Half of Stages):**
> At the outset, [raw material / input] is [collected / harvested / extracted] from [source]. Subsequently, it is [transported to / transferred to] a [facility / plant / chamber] where it is [processed / heated / compressed / filtered]. Once this stage is complete, the [intermediate product] is [subjected to / passed through] [next step], which [serves to / functions to] [purpose of this step].

**Body 2 (Second Half of Stages):**
> Following this, the [intermediate product] is [further refined / assembled / packaged / treated] at [location / in a dedicated unit]. [It is then tested / inspected / quality-checked] before being [dispatched / distributed / released]. In the final stage, [finished product] is [delivered to / made available to / stored in] [destination]. [If cyclical: At this point, [by-product / waste] is [recycled / returned to] the beginning of the process, allowing the cycle to recommence.]

---

**Process Verbs (Passive):**
is extracted, is harvested, is collected, is transported, is conveyed, is subjected to, is heated, is cooled, is compressed, is filtered, is purified, is refined, is assembled, is moulded, is dried, is packaged, is stored, is dispatched, is distributed, is discarded, is recycled, is discharged, is converted into, is blended with

### CATEGORY 4: PICTURE / OBJECT DESCRIPTION

**When to use:** Rare in IELTS, but can appear. A diagram showing the parts of an object, machine, or structure.

**Adaptation:** Use the Process Template structure. Introduction paraphrases the prompt. Overview states the number of main components and their general function. Body 1 and Body 2 each describe a subset of components with their positions and functions.

**Key Vocabulary Additions:**
is composed of, consists of, is equipped with, is fitted with, is attached to, is connected to, is mounted on, is housed within, is positioned at the [top/base/centre], serves the function of, is designed to

## A.4 Worked Example: Line Graph (Data with Time Axis)

**Question:** "The line graph below shows the percentage of tourists to Scotland who visited four different attractions between 1980 and 2010."

---

**Introduction:**
The line graph compares the proportion of tourists visiting four attractions in Scotland over a 30-year period, from 1980 to 2010.

**Overview:**
Overall, the Edinburgh Castle remained the most popular attraction throughout the entire period, despite a slight decline toward the end. In contrast, the Aquarium witnessed the most dramatic growth, rising from the least visited attraction to the second most popular by 2010.

**Body 1:**
In 1980, Edinburgh Castle attracted approximately 35% of all tourists, making it the most visited site by a considerable margin. Over the following two decades, this figure climbed steadily to peak at around 45% in 2000, before easing to 40% by 2010. The Science Museum, which began at a comparable level of roughly 30%, followed a broadly similar trajectory, rising to 35% in 1995, though it subsequently declined to approximately 25% by the end of the period.

**Body 2:**
Turning to the remaining attractions, the Aquarium started at a mere 10% in 1980 but surged consistently to reach 30% by 2010, overtaking the Science Museum in the process. Meanwhile, the Botanical Gardens fluctuated between 15% and 20% throughout the period, showing no discernible long-term trend and remaining the least visited attraction by 2010.

## A.5 Worked Example: Process Diagram

**Question:** "The diagram below shows how orange juice is produced."

---

**Introduction:**
The diagram illustrates the process by which oranges are harvested and converted into packaged juice for commercial sale.

**Overview:**
Overall, the process comprises eight distinct stages, commencing with the picking of ripe oranges from trees and culminating in the delivery of the finished product to supermarkets. It is a linear process that involves both manual and mechanised operations.

**Body 1:**
At the outset, ripe oranges are picked from trees either by hand or with the aid of mechanical harvesters. Subsequently, the fruit is transported by truck to a processing plant, where it is washed and sorted to remove any damaged or undersized oranges. Once this stage is complete, the oranges are fed into an extraction machine where the juice is separated from the peel and pulp.

**Body 2:**
Following extraction, the juice is filtered to remove any remaining solids and is then pasteurised at high temperature to eliminate bacteria. At the next stage, the pasteurised juice is transferred to a filling machine, where it is poured into cartons and sealed. In the final stage, the packaged juice is stored in a refrigerated warehouse before being dispatched to supermarkets and retail outlets for sale.

## A.6 Worked Example: Map

**Question:** "The maps below show the village of Norbiton in 1900 and in the present day."

---

**Introduction:**
The two maps compare the layout of the village of Norbiton in 1900 with its present-day configuration.

**Overview:**
Overall, Norbiton underwent substantial transformation over the period, evolving from a small, rural settlement into a developed residential area. The most notable changes include the construction of new housing estates and the replacement of farmland with recreational facilities.

**Body 1:**
In 1900, Norbiton was characterised by a handful of houses clustered around a central crossroads. To the north of the village, a large area of farmland stretched across the landscape, while woodland occupied the south-western section. A single narrow road connected the village to the main road situated to the east, and a small church was positioned adjacent to the crossroads.

**Body 2:**
By the present day, the farmland to the north had been entirely replaced by a large housing estate, and the woodland in the south-west had been cleared to make way for a sports ground and a children's playground. The original narrow road was widened and extended, with several new access roads constructed to serve the expanded residential areas. The church, however, remained in its original position, retaining its status as a central landmark within the village.

# PART B: TASK 2 -- ESSAY (40 minutes, 250+ words)

---

## B.1 Universal Rules (Apply to Every Task 2 Response)

**Default Structure: 4 Paragraphs**

| Paragraph     | Function                                            | Sentences | Key Rule                                                    |
|:--------------|:----------------------------------------------------|:---------:|:------------------------------------------------------------|
| Introduction  | Paraphrase the prompt + state your thesis clearly    | 2-3       | The examiner must know your position by the end of the introduction. |
| Body 1        | Your primary argument or the first perspective       | 5-7       | Topic sentence, explanation, example, result.               |
| Body 2        | Your secondary argument, concession, or the second perspective | 5-7 | Same internal structure. If this is a concession paragraph, keep it shorter (3-4 sentences) and explicitly rebut. |
| Conclusion    | Restate thesis + summarise + final thought           | 2-3       | Never introduce a new idea. Mirror the introduction using different words. |

**When to Use 5 Paragraphs:**
A 5-paragraph structure is justified only when the question explicitly requires you to address two distinct sub-questions (e.g., "What are the causes? What solutions can be proposed?") AND you want to give your opinion in a separate paragraph. In all other cases, 4 paragraphs is tighter and scores equally well.

The 5-paragraph variant:
- Introduction
- Body 1 (Sub-question 1 or View A)
- Body 2 (Sub-question 2 or View B)
- Body 3 (Your independent opinion, kept to 3-4 sentences)
- Conclusion

**Word Count Strategy:**
- Introduction: 40-50 words
- Body 1: 80-100 words
- Body 2: 80-100 words
- Conclusion: 30-40 words
- Total: 270-300 words (the optimal range; going beyond 300 risks more errors under time pressure)

**The PEEL Structure for Every Body Paragraph:**
- **P** -- Point (Topic Sentence): State the paragraph's main idea in one clear sentence.
- **E** -- Explanation: Elaborate on why this point is true or how it operates.
- **E** -- Example: Provide a specific, concrete example. Use "For instance," or "A compelling illustration of this is" or "This is evidenced by."
- **L** -- Link: Connect back to the thesis or transition to the next paragraph. Use "Consequently," or "This demonstrates that" or "As a result,".

## B.2 Task 2 Templates by Essay Type

---

### TYPE 1: AGREE / DISAGREE

**Prompt Pattern:** "To what extent do you agree or disagree?" or "Do you agree or disagree?"

**Strategic Decision:** Always take a clear position. "Completely agree" or "Completely disagree" is straightforward and effective to execute. If you wish to demonstrate nuanced critical thinking, you can adopt a "largely agree/disagree" stance and dedicate a structured Body 2 to the concession and rebuttal.

---

#### TEMPLATE 1A: STRONG POSITION (4 paragraphs -- recommended default)

Two body paragraphs supporting your position. The concession is woven into the conclusion.

**Introduction:**
> [Paraphrase the statement]. I [completely / firmly] [agree / disagree] with this view, as [reason 1 in brief], and moreover, [reason 2 in brief].

**Body 1 (Reason 1):**
> The [most / primary] compelling reason to [support / reject] this position is that [topic sentence]. [Explanation: 1-2 sentences]. For instance, [specific example]. [Consequently / As a result], [link back to thesis].

**Body 2 (Reason 2):**
> [Furthermore / Equally important], [topic sentence for reason 2]. [Explanation]. [A notable example of this can be seen in / This is well-exemplified by] [example]. [This further reinforces the argument that / It follows, therefore, that] [link back to thesis].

**Conclusion:**
> In conclusion, [although / while] [brief acknowledgement of the other side, 1 clause], the evidence [overwhelmingly / convincingly] suggests that [restate thesis in different words]. [Final thought: recommendation or future implication].

---

#### TEMPLATE 1B: NUANCED POSITION (4 paragraphs -- Concession & Counter-Argument Model)

Two body paragraphs: one strong paragraph for your position, one shorter concession paragraph, followed by a rebuttal that reinforces your original stance.

**Introduction:**
> [Paraphrase the statement]. While there are [valid / understandable] reasons to [hold the opposite view], I [largely / predominantly] [agree / disagree] with this assertion.

**Body 1 (Your Position -- longer, 5-7 sentences):**
> The [most persuasive / strongest] argument [in favour of / against] this view is that [topic sentence]. [Explanation: 2 sentences]. For instance, [detailed example]. [Consequently / Therefore], [link to thesis].

**Body 2 (Concession + Rebuttal -- shorter, 3-4 sentences):**
> Admittedly, [opponents / proponents] of this view may argue that [counter-argument in 1 sentence]. [Brief acknowledgement that this has some validity]. Nevertheless, [this concern is [overstated / outweighed] because [rebuttal in 1-2 sentences], which [ultimately / in effect] [strengthens / does not diminish] the case for [your position]].

**Conclusion:**
> In conclusion, despite [the partial validity of the opposing perspective], I maintain that [restate thesis]. [Final thought].

### TYPE 2: DISCUSSION + OPINION

**Prompt Pattern:** "Discuss both views and give your own opinion." or "Some people think X. Others believe Y. Discuss both views and give your opinion."

**Strategic Note:** You must discuss BOTH sides. Your opinion can be embedded in the introduction and conclusion (no need for a separate opinion paragraph). This keeps the response to 4 clean paragraphs.

---

#### DISCUSSION TEMPLATE (4 paragraphs)

**Introduction:**
> [Paraphrase the two views]. While both perspectives have merit, I [am more inclined toward / believe that] [your preferred view] [because brief reason].

**Body 1 (View A):**
> On the one hand, proponents of [View A] argue that [topic sentence]. [Explanation]. For instance, [example]. [As a result / Consequently], [link: why this view has some validity].

**Body 2 (View B -- your preferred view, develop more fully):**
> On the other hand, [advocates of View B / those who contend that] [topic sentence]. [Explanation: 2 sentences]. [A notable illustration of this is / This can be observed in] [example]. [This suggests that / It is therefore evident that] [link to your opinion].

**Conclusion:**
> In conclusion, although [View A raises legitimate concerns], I am of the firm opinion that [View B] is the [more compelling / more sustainable / more equitable] position because [brief restatement of key reason]. [Final thought].

---

### TYPE 3: ADVANTAGES AND DISADVANTAGES

**Prompt Pattern:** "What are the advantages and disadvantages of X?" or "Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?"

**Important Distinction:**
- "Discuss advantages and disadvantages" = Present both sides. You may or may not give an opinion.
- "Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?" = You MUST give a clear opinion on which side is stronger.

---

#### TEMPLATE 3A: STANDARD (Discuss both sides)

**Introduction:**
> [Paraphrase the topic]. While [topic] offers [certain / notable] benefits, it also presents [significant / non-trivial] drawbacks. This essay will examine both sides.

**Body 1 (Advantages):**
> One of the [primary / most significant] advantages of [topic] is that [topic sentence]. [Explanation]. For instance, [example]. [Furthermore / In addition], [second advantage in 1-2 sentences].

**Body 2 (Disadvantages):**
> [However / Conversely], [topic] also carries [considerable / notable] disadvantages. [Chief / Foremost] among these is that [topic sentence]. [Explanation]. [A case in point is / This is evidenced by] [example]. [Moreover], [second disadvantage in 1-2 sentences].

**Conclusion:**
> In conclusion, [topic] is a [nuanced / multifaceted] issue that presents both [opportunities and challenges / merits and limitations]. [Optional opinion: On balance, I believe that [advantages / disadvantages] [carry greater weight / are more consequential] because [reason].]

---

#### TEMPLATE 3B: OUTWEIGH (You must take a position)

**Introduction:**
> [Paraphrase the topic]. Although [topic] [has certain drawbacks / entails some risks], I [firmly / strongly] believe that its advantages [far outweigh / significantly eclipse] its disadvantages.

**Body 1 (Your dominant side -- longer, 5-7 sentences):**
> The [most compelling / principal] benefit of [topic] is that [topic sentence]. [Explanation: 2 sentences]. For instance, [example]. [In addition / Beyond this], [second advantage]. [Collectively / Taken together], these benefits [demonstrate / underscore] [why the positive impact is substantial].

**Body 2 (Concession side -- shorter, 3-4 sentences + solution/rebuttal):**
> Admittedly, [one notable drawback / a frequently cited concern] is that [disadvantage]. [Brief elaboration]. However, this can be [effectively mitigated / addressed] through [solution], which [reduces / neutralises] the [severity / impact] of this limitation.

**Conclusion:**
> In conclusion, while [topic] is not without its [shortcomings / limitations], the [tangible / demonstrable] benefits it offers [in terms of X and Y] [clearly / decisively] outweigh the potential drawbacks. [Final thought: recommendation or forward-looking statement].

---

### TYPE 4: CAUSES/PROBLEMS AND SOLUTIONS (or REASONS AND SOLUTIONS)

**Prompt Pattern:** "What are the causes and solutions?" or "What problems does this cause, and what measures can be taken?"

**Strategic Note:** This is factual and objective. You do not need to give a personal opinion unless the question asks for one. Use a paired structure: one cause paired with one solution within each body paragraph, or dedicate Body 1 to causes and Body 2 to solutions.

---

#### TEMPLATE 4A: PAIRED STRUCTURE (cause + solution together -- recommended)

**Introduction:**
> [Paraphrase the issue]. This [trend / problem / phenomenon] can be attributed to [several / two primary] factors, though [effective / viable] solutions exist to [address / mitigate / counteract] them.

**Body 1 (Cause 1 + Solution 1):**
> [The most significant / One of the primary] [cause(s) / reason(s)] for this issue is [topic sentence]. [Explanation: 1-2 sentences]. For instance, [example]. To address this, [solution]. [This would / This measure could] [reduce / alleviate / prevent] [the problem] by [mechanism].

**Body 2 (Cause 2 + Solution 2):**
> [Another contributing factor / A further cause] is [topic sentence]. [Explanation]. [This is evidenced by / A clear example of this is] [example]. [A practical remedy for this would be / One effective countermeasure is] [solution]. [If implemented effectively / Should this be adopted], it would [outcome].

**Conclusion:**
> In summary, while [issue] presents a [formidable / complex] challenge [driven by / rooted in] [brief restatement of causes], [implementing / adopting] measures such as [brief restatement of solutions] would [significantly / meaningfully] [ameliorate / alleviate] the situation. [Final thought].

#### TEMPLATE 4B: SEPARATED STRUCTURE (all causes, then all solutions)

**Introduction:**
> Same as Template 4A.

**Body 1 (All Causes/Problems):**
> The [root / principal] cause of this [phenomenon / issue] can be attributed to [cause 1]. [Explanation + example]. [Compounding this / Exacerbating the situation], [cause 2]. [Explanation + brief example].

**Body 2 (All Solutions):**
> Nevertheless, this issue is [far from / not] insurmountable. [The most effective measure would be / A viable first step is] [solution 1]. [Explanation of how it works]. [In tandem with this / Complementing this approach], [solution 2] could be [implemented / introduced] to [outcome].

**Conclusion:**
> Same structure as Template 4A.

### TYPE 5: TWO-PART / DIRECT QUESTION

**Prompt Pattern:** "Why is this the case? Is this a positive or negative development?" or "What are the reasons for this? What effect does it have?"

**Strategic Note:** Each body paragraph answers one question. Both must be addressed fully and explicitly.

---

#### TWO-PART QUESTION TEMPLATE (4 paragraphs)

**Introduction:**
> [Paraphrase the topic and both questions]. This essay will first examine [the reasons behind / why] [Q1 topic] before [assessing whether / evaluating how] [Q2 topic].

**Body 1 (Answer to Question 1):**
> [The primary reason for / The most likely explanation for] this [trend / development] is that [topic sentence]. [Explanation]. For instance, [example]. [A secondary factor / In addition] is [second reason + brief support].

**Body 2 (Answer to Question 2):**
> [Regarding whether this is positive or negative / As for the effects], I believe this is [largely positive / predominantly negative / a mixed development]. [Topic sentence for main effect]. [Explanation + example]. [Consequently], [link to assessment].

**Conclusion:**
> In conclusion, [Q1 answer restated briefly] and [Q2 answer restated briefly]. [Final thought or recommendation].

---

### TYPE 6: WHICH DO YOU PREFER? / COMPARATIVE

**Prompt Pattern:** "Some people prefer X while others prefer Y. Which do you prefer?" or "Which approach is more effective?"

**Strategic Note:** Take a clear position. Structure is identical to Agree/Disagree Template 1A, with one body paragraph for your preferred option and one for the alternative (treated as a concession).

---

#### PREFERENCE TEMPLATE (4 paragraphs)

**Introduction:**
> [Paraphrase the two options]. While both [approaches / options] have [their respective merits], I [strongly / firmly] prefer [option X] because [brief reason].

**Body 1 (Your preferred option -- developed fully):**
> The [primary / most persuasive] reason for favouring [option X] is that [topic sentence]. [Explanation]. For instance, [example]. [This demonstrates that / As a result], [link to thesis].

**Body 2 (Alternative option -- concession + rebuttal):**
> Admittedly, [option Y] does offer [advantage in 1 sentence]. [However / Nevertheless], [limitation or rebuttal]. [When weighed against the benefits of option X / In comparison], [option Y] [falls short / proves less effective] because [reason].

**Conclusion:**
> In conclusion, although [option Y] has [some appeal / certain advantages], [option X] is [the more effective / the preferable] choice for [reason]. [Final thought].

## B.6 Worked Example: Agree/Disagree (Nuanced Position)

**Question:** "Some people believe that universities should focus only on academic subjects. Others think they should also teach practical skills for employment. To what extent do you agree or disagree?"

---

**Introduction:**
It is frequently argued that university education should be restricted to purely academic disciplines. While academic knowledge undoubtedly forms the foundation of higher education, I largely disagree with this view, as equipping students with practical, employment-oriented skills is equally essential in the modern economy.

**Body 1 (Practical skills are essential -- main argument):**
The most compelling reason for incorporating practical skills into university curricula is that graduates increasingly face a competitive labour market that demands more than theoretical knowledge. Employers consistently report that candidates who possess transferable competencies such as communication, data analysis, and project management are significantly more employable than those with purely academic qualifications. For instance, a survey conducted by the Graduate Recruitment Bureau in 2022 found that 78% of employers ranked practical experience and applicable skills above degree classification when making hiring decisions. Consequently, universities that neglect this dimension of education risk producing graduates who are ill-prepared for the workforce.

**Body 2 (Concession + rebuttal):**
Admittedly, proponents of a purely academic approach argue that universities exist primarily to advance knowledge and cultivate intellectual inquiry, which should not be diluted by vocational training. There is some validity to this concern, particularly in research-intensive disciplines. Nevertheless, this perspective overlooks the fact that practical skills and academic rigour are not mutually exclusive; they can coexist within a well-designed curriculum without compromising scholarly standards.

**Conclusion:**
In conclusion, although preserving academic depth remains a legitimate priority, I maintain that universities must also prepare students for the realities of professional life. A balanced approach that integrates both academic and practical components would serve students, employers, and society most effectively.

## B.7 Worked Example: Causes and Solutions (Paired Structure)

**Question:** "In many countries, the number of people suffering from stress-related illnesses is increasing. What are the causes of this, and what solutions can be proposed?"

---

**Introduction:**
In recent years, the prevalence of stress-related illnesses has risen markedly across both developed and developing nations. This trend can be attributed to several interconnected factors, though effective measures can be taken to counteract it.

**Body 1 (Cause 1 + Solution 1):**
The most significant cause of this increase is the intensification of workplace demands. In an increasingly competitive global economy, employees are routinely expected to work longer hours, meet tighter deadlines, and remain digitally available outside of working hours. For instance, a study by the World Health Organisation revealed that individuals working more than 55 hours per week face a 35% higher risk of cardiovascular disease. To address this, governments could implement and enforce statutory limits on working hours, while employers could introduce flexible scheduling and mandatory rest periods, thereby reducing chronic occupational stress.

**Body 2 (Cause 2 + Solution 2):**
A further contributing factor is the erosion of social support networks in modern urban environments. As people relocate for career opportunities, they often leave behind family and community ties, leading to social isolation and heightened vulnerability to mental health disorders. A practical remedy for this would be increased public investment in community centres, counselling services, and peer-support programmes. Should such initiatives be adopted at a municipal level, they would provide accessible outlets for individuals experiencing early symptoms of stress.

**Conclusion:**
In summary, while the rise in stress-related illness presents a complex public health challenge driven by workplace pressure and social fragmentation, implementing targeted policy reforms and community-based interventions would meaningfully alleviate the situation.
