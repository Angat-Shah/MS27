# IELTS T2|06 – Mobile Phones in Public Places

**“Some people think mobile phones should be banned in public places such as in libraries, shops and public transport. To what extent do you agree or disagree?”**

Word Count: **323**

---

## Essay

Mobile phones have become an indispensable part of modern life, enabling people to communicate, access information, and perform daily tasks with ease. While some argue that these devices should be prohibited in public places, I partially disagree with a complete ban. Instead, I believe that their use should be regulated according to the nature of the location.

To begin with, imposing a complete ban on mobile phones in all public places would be impractical and unnecessary. For instance, in shops, smartphones have become an essential tool for comparing prices, checking product reviews, making cashless payments, and communicating with family members before making purchases. Likewise, passengers on public transport often rely on their phones for navigation, electronic tickets, and travel updates. Preventing people from using their phones in these situations would create inconvenience without offering any significant benefit.

Furthermore, cell phones play a crucial role in ensuring personal safety. Individuals may need to call emergency services, inform relatives about unexpected circumstances, or access important health information at any time. A total ban could therefore put people at unnecessary risk. Rather than prohibiting phones altogether, governments and public institutions should promote responsible behavior by encouraging users to keep their devices on silent mode, avoid speaking loudly, and respect those around them.

While the above perspective has merit, there are certain public places where restrictions on mobile phone use are entirely justified. Libraries, for instance, are intended to provide a quiet environment for reading and studying. Loud conversations, notification sounds, or videos played without headphones can easily distract others and reduce the effectiveness of these shared spaces.

In conclusion, although restrictions on mobile phone use are appropriate in places where silence is essential, I do not believe they should be completely banned from all public spaces. A balanced perspective would be to encourage individuals to use mobile phones considerately in public places so that they can benefit from the conveniences that phones provide without disrupting others.
