## Report

The bar chart illustrates the population growth of the world’s largest cities between 1970 and 2015. It also compares the proportion of people living in urban and rural areas over the same period.

Overall, it is evident that the populations of all five cities increased significantly during the period. Tokyo remained the largest city throughout, while Jakarta experienced the fastest growth. In addition, the number of people living in urban areas experienced a rapid increase around 2000, whereas the rural population stabilized after 1990.

In 1970, Tokyo had the highest population, at around 16 million, followed by New York with approximately 14 million. Shanghai had a population of about 12 million, Bombay had a population of about 5 million, while Jakarta was the smallest city, with roughly 3 million inhabitants. By 2015, Tokyo's population had grown to nearly 29 million, maintaining its leading position. Jakarta showed the most dramatic increase, reaching around 20 million and overtaking New York. Bombay and Shanghai also recorded substantial growth, while New York experienced the slowest increase among the five cities.

The ranking of the world's five largest cities also changed considerably over the period. In 1950, the top five cities were New York, London, Tokyo, Paris, and Moscow. However, by 2010, Tokyo had become the largest city, while Bombay, Shanghai, and Jakarta entered the list, replacing London, Paris, and Moscow. This reflects the rapid growth of Asian cities over the period.

The line graph indicates a clear shift from rural to urban living. In 1970, the majority of the population lived in rural areas, accounting for about 3 billion of the world's population, compared with only 1.5 billion in urban regions. However, the population of urban residents increased steadily throughout the period, surpassing the rural population around the late 1990s. By 2015, approximately 5 billion people lived in urban areas, while the rural figure remained stable at about 3.2 billion.
