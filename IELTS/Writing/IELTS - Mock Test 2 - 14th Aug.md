## Task 1 – Brazilian Amazon Deforestation Trends & Causes

**“The line chart below shows the rates of deforestation in the Brazilian Amazon for the years 2008 to 1017 and the pie chart below shows the causes for deforestation in the Brazilian Amazon for the same years. Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.”**

Word Count: **188**
Score: **2.0**

---

### Report

The line graph illustrates changes in the area of the Brazilian Amazon affected by deforestation between 2008 and 2017, while the pie chart shows the proportions accounted for by five different causes of deforestation over the same period.

Overall, deforestation declined substantially despite considerable fluctuations during the first six years. Small-holder agriculture was the largest contributor, whereas other causes accounted for only a small minority of the total.

In 2008, approximately 34,000 square kilometers of forest were cleared. This figure fell slightly to 32,000 in 2009, before dropping sharply to about 22,000 in 2010. It then rose to roughly 27,000 in 2011, followed by another decline to 16,000 in 2012. Deforestation peaked again at around 29,000 square kilometres in 2013. Thereafter, the figure decreased dramatically, reaching approximately 14,000 in 2014, 6,000 in 2015 and just 2,000 in 2016, before edging up to 3,000 in 2017.

Regarding the causes, small-holder agriculture represented 35% of deforestation, followed by cattle pasture at 25%. Large-scale agriculture and logging accounted for 20% and 15% respectively, while other causes made up the remaining 5%.

## Task 2 – Physical Inactivity Across Generations

**“Too many young people and too many old people do not exercise enough. What is causing this? What can be done to change this?”**

Word Count: **293**
Score: **4.0**

---

### Essay

In the contemporary epoch, sedentary lifestyles are increasingly pervasive among both the youth and the elderly. This is mainly due to the ubiquity of technological distractions and a lack of suitable facilities for the aged. However, several measures can be taken to encourage greater physical activity.

One compelling reason is the amount of time people spend sitting. Young people are often attracted to smartphones, video games and virtual environments, while adults may spend most of their working hours at a desk. Additionally, exercise is frequently considered an optional activity rather than an integral part of daily life. Consequently, people often choose convenient alternatives, such as driving instead of walking. To address this issue, schools should give greater priority to physical education and provide enjoyable activities that encourage students to participate in sports. Governments should also develop safe walking and cycling paths and provide affordable public sports facilities. Such measures could make physical activity a more natural and regular part of people's lives.

Another reason is that senior citizens may become less active after retirement because they have fewer daily responsibilities and fear physical strain or injury. This problem is further exacerbated by a lack of suitable facilities for the aged. For elderly people, communities could therefore establish age-appropriate spaces and organise low-impact activities such as walking groups or yoga classes. Such initiatives would not only improve physical health but also reduce social isolation. In addition, incentives such as tax rebates for gym memberships could motivate people to exercise regularly.

In conclusion, insufficient exercise is largely caused by sedentary lifestyles, technological distractions and inadequate facilities. By combining effective public policy with individual initiative, schools, governments and communities can create an environment in which physical activity becomes a natural and regular part of people's lives.
