### Question 1
$$y < -6$$

| Quantity A | Quantity B |
| :---: | :---: |
| $y$ | $-5$ |

- [A] Quantity A is greater.
- [B] Quantity B is greater.
- [C] The two quantities are equal.
- [D] The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[B] Quantity B is greater.**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. We are given the inequality $y < -6$.
> 2. On a standard real number line, numbers positioned further to the right are greater.
> 3. Comparing $-6$ and $-5$:
>    $$-6 < -5$$
> 4. By the transitive property of inequality:
>    $$y < -6 < -5 \implies y < -5$$
> 5. Therefore, $\text{Quantity B} > \text{Quantity A}$.

### Question 2

- [C] The two quantities are equal.
- [D] The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.

<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**

> 2. The set contains $5$ numbers. When arranged in ascending order, the median is the 3rd number:

### Question 3
$r$ and $t$ are consecutive integers, and $p = r^2 + t$.

| Quantity A | Quantity B |
| :---: | :---: |
| $(-1)^p$ | $-1$ |

- [A] Quantity A is greater.
- [B] Quantity B is greater.
- [C] The two quantities are equal.
- [D] The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[C] The two quantities are equal.**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Because $r$ and $t$ are consecutive integers, one must be even and the other must be odd.
> 2. Test both parity possibilities for $r$ and $t$:
>    - **Case 1: $r$ is even, $t$ is odd.**  
>      $r^2 = (\text{even})^2 = \text{even}$.  
>      $p = r^2 + t = \text{even} + \text{odd} = \text{odd}$.
>    - **Case 2: $r$ is odd, $t$ is even.**  
>      $r^2 = (\text{odd})^2 = \text{odd}$.  
>      $p = r^2 + t = \text{odd} + \text{even} = \text{odd}$.
> 3. In both possible cases, $p$ is guaranteed to be an **odd integer**.
> 4. Evaluating $(-1)^p$ for any odd integer power $p$:
>    $$(-1)^{\text{odd}} = -1$$
> 5. Therefore, $\text{Quantity A} = -1$ and $\text{Quantity B} = -1$. The two quantities are equal.

</details>

### Question 5
The sum of the annual salaries of the $21$ teachers at School X is $\$781,200$.  
Twelve of the $21$ teachers have an annual salary that is less than $\$37,000$.

| Quantity A | Quantity B |
| :---: | :---: |
| The average (arithmetic mean) of the annual salaries of the $21$ teachers | The median of the annual salaries of the $21$ teachers |

- [A] Quantity A is greater.
- [B] Quantity B is greater.
- [C] The two quantities are equal.
- [D] The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[A] Quantity A is greater.**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Calculate **Quantity A** (the arithmetic mean):
>    $$\text{Mean} = \frac{\text{Total Sum}}{\text{Number of Teachers}} = \frac{781,200}{21} = \$37,200$$
> 2. Evaluate **Quantity B** (the median):
>    - For $21$ salaries arranged in non-decreasing order:
>      $$s_1 \le s_2 \le \dots \le s_{11} \le \dots \le s_{21}$$
>    - The median is the 11th salary ($s_{11}$).
> 3. We are told that $12$ teachers earn strictly less than $\$37,000$.
>    - This means the first $12$ salaries in the ordered list must satisfy:
>      $$s_1, s_2, \dots, s_{11}, s_{12} < \$37,000$$
>    - In particular, the 11th salary must be less than $\$37,000$:
>      $$\text{Median} = s_{11} < \$37,000$$
> 4. Compare the two quantities:
>    $$\text{Quantity A} = \$37,200 > \$37,000 > \text{Quantity B}$$
> 5. Therefore, **Quantity A is greater**.

</details>

### Question 6
In the $xy$-plane, point $(c, d)$ lies in the third quadrant. The line segment connecting the origin $(0,0)$ to $(c, d)$ forms an angle of $44^\circ$ with the negative $y$-axis.

| Quantity A | Quantity B |
| :---: | :---: |
| $c$ | $d$ |

- [A] Quantity A is greater.
- [B] Quantity B is greater.
- [C] The two quantities are equal.
- [D] The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[A] Quantity A is greater.**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Point $(c, d)$ lies in Quadrant III, which means both coordinates are strictly negative:
>    $$c < 0 \quad \text{and} \quad d < 0$$
> 2. Consider the right triangle formed by the origin, $(0, d)$, and $(c, d)$:
>    - The vertical side along the negative $y$-axis has length $|d| = -d$.
>    - The horizontal side parallel to the $x$-axis has length $|c| = -c$.
>    - The angle between the hypotenuse and the negative $y$-axis is $44^\circ$.
> 3. Using trigonometry:
>    $$\tan(44^\circ) = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \frac{|c|}{|d|}$$
> 4. Since $44^\circ < 45^\circ$ and the tangent function is strictly increasing on $(0^\circ, 90^\circ)$:
>    $$\tan(44^\circ) < \tan(45^\circ) = 1 \implies \frac{|c|}{|d|} < 1 \implies |c| < |d|$$
> 5. Because both numbers are negative, having a smaller absolute value corresponds to being greater (closer to zero):
>    $$|c| < |d| \implies -c < -d \implies c > d$$
>    *(For example, if $|c| \approx 0.966$ and $|d| = 1$, then $c = -0.966$ and $d = -1.0$. Clearly $-0.966 > -1.0$).*
> 6. Therefore, **Quantity A is greater**.

</details>

### Question 9

| Quantity A | Quantity B |
| :---: | :---: |
| The length of a leg of an isosceles right triangle with area $R$ | The length of a side of a square with area $R$ |

- [A] Quantity A is greater.
- [B] Quantity B is greater.
- [C] The two quantities are equal.
- [D] The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[A] Quantity A is greater.**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Let $a$ be the length of each leg of the isosceles right triangle.
>    - The area of the triangle is:
>      $$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}a^2 = R \implies a^2 = 2R \implies a = \sqrt{2R} = \sqrt{2}\sqrt{R}$$
>    - Thus, $\text{Quantity A} = \sqrt{2}\sqrt{R}$.
> 2. Let $s$ be the side length of the square with area $R$.
>    - The area of the square is:
>      $$\text{Area} = s^2 = R \implies s = \sqrt{R}$$
>    - Thus, $\text{Quantity B} = \sqrt{R}$.
> 3. Because $R > 0$ and $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414 > 1$:
>    $$\sqrt{2}\sqrt{R} > \sqrt{R} \implies \text{Quantity A} > \text{Quantity B}$$
> 4. Therefore, **Quantity A is greater**.

</details>

The relationship between temperature $C$, in degrees Celsius, and temperature $F$, in degrees Fahrenheit, is given by the formula:

If a recipe calls for an oven temperature of $210$ degrees Celsius, what is the oven temperature in degrees Fahrenheit?

- [A] $320$
- [B] $390$
- [C] $410$
- [D] $420$
- [E] $450$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[C] $410$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Substitute $C = 210$ into the temperature conversion formula:
>    $$F = \frac{9}{5}(210) + 32$$
> 2. Simplify the multiplication first:
>    $$\frac{210}{5} = 42$$

> 3. Add $32$:
>    $$F = 378 + 32 = 410^\circ\text{F}$$

### Question 11
Of the students in a school, $20$ percent are in the science club and $30$ percent are in the band. If $25$ percent of the students in the school are in the band but are not in the science club, what percent of the students who are in the science club are not in the band?

- [A] $5\%$
- [B] $20\%$
- [C] $25\%$
- [D] $60\%$
- [E] $75\%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[E] $75\%$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Let $T = 100$ be the total number of students in the school.
>    - Science club: $S = 20\% \times 100 = 20$.
>    - Band: $B = 30\% \times 100 = 30$.
> 2. We are given that students in the band who are **not** in the science club ($B \setminus S$) represent $25\%$ of the school:
>    $$B \setminus S = 25$$
> 3. The total number of band members is $B = (B \cap S) + (B \setminus S)$:
>    $$30 = (B \cap S) + 25 \implies B \cap S = 5$$
>    *(So $5$ students are in both the science club and the band).*
> 4. Find the number of science club members who are **not** in the band ($S \setminus B$):
>    $$S \setminus B = S - (B \cap S) = 20 - 5 = 15$$
> 5. The question asks: *\"what percent of the students who are in the science club are not in the band?\"*
>    $$\text{Required Percentage} = \frac{S \setminus B}{S} \times 100\% = \frac{15}{20} \times 100\% = \frac{3}{4} \times 100\% = 75\%$$

</details>

### Question 12
Each year, the members of a book club select novels and nonfiction books to read. The club meets $3$ times to discuss each novel and $5$ times to discuss each nonfiction book they select. Last year, the club met $52$ times and discussed $12$ books. How many novels did the club discuss last year?

- [A] $4$
- [B] $7$
- [C] $8$
- [D] $10$
- [E] $14$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[A] $4$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Let $n$ be the number of novels discussed, and $b$ be the number of nonfiction books discussed.
> 2. Set up a system of linear equations from the problem statement:
>    - Total books:
>      $$n + b = 12 \implies b = 12 - n$$
>    - Total club meetings:
>      $$3n + 5b = 52$$
> 3. Substitute $b = 12 - n$ into the meetings equation:
>    $$3n + 5(12 - n) = 52$$
>    $$3n + 60 - 5n = 52$$
>    $$-2n = 52 - 60 = -8$$
>    $$n = 4$$
> 4. Verify: $4$ novels ($4 \times 3 = 12$ meetings) and $8$ nonfiction ($8 \times 5 = 40$ meetings) gives $12 + 40 = 52$ meetings and $4 + 8 = 12$ books.

</details>

### Question 13
If $-1 < x < y < 0$, which of the following shows the expressions $x^2 y$, $x y$, and $x y^2$ listed in order from least to greatest?

- [A] $xy, x^2 y, xy^2$
- [B] $xy, xy^2, x^2 y$
- [C] $xy^2, xy, x^2 y$
- [D] $xy^2, x^2 y, xy$
- [E] $x^2 y, xy^2, xy$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[E] $x^2 y, xy^2, xy$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. We are given $-1 < x < y < 0$.
>    - Both $x$ and $y$ are negative fractions between $-1$ and $0$.
>    - Since $x < y$, $x$ is further to the left on the number line, meaning $|x| > |y|$.
> 2. Determine the signs of each expression:
>    - $xy$: $(\text{negative}) \times (\text{negative}) > 0$ (**strictly positive**).
>    - $x^2 y$: $x^2 > 0$ and $y < 0 \implies x^2 y < 0$ (**strictly negative**).
>    - $xy^2$: $x < 0$ and $y^2 > 0 \implies xy^2 < 0$ (**strictly negative**).
> 3. Since $xy > 0$, it is the greatest of the three expressions.
> 4. Compare the two negative quantities $x^2 y$ and $xy^2$:
>    - Factor out $xy$:
>      $$x^2 y = x(xy) \quad \text{and} \quad xy^2 = y(xy)$$
>    - Since $x < y$ and we know $xy > 0$, multiplying the inequality $x < y$ by the positive number $xy$ preserves the inequality direction:
>      $$x(xy) < y(xy) \implies x^2 y < xy^2$$
> 5. Combining the terms from least to greatest:
>    $$x^2 y < xy^2 < xy$$

</details>

### Question 14
The $5$ letters in the list $G, H, I, J, K$ are to be rearranged so that $G$ is the 3rd letter in the list and $H$ is not next to $G$. How many such rearrangements are there?

- [A] $6$
- [B] $12$
- [C] $18$
- [D] $24$
- [E] $36$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[B] $12$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. We have $5$ positions: $\_ \quad \_ \quad \_ \quad \_ \quad \_$.
> 2. Condition 1: $G$ must occupy position 3:
>    $$\text{Position 1} \quad \text{Position 2} \quad \mathbf{G} \quad \text{Position 4} \quad \text{Position 5}$$
> 3. Condition 2: $H$ cannot be adjacent to $G$, meaning $H$ cannot be placed in Position 2 or Position 4.
>    - Therefore, $H$ must be placed in either **Position 1** or **Position 5** ($2$ choices).
> 4. Once $H$ is placed:
>    - The remaining $3$ letters ($I, J, K$) can be arranged in the remaining $3$ open positions in $3!$ ways:
>      $$3! = 3 \times 2 \times 1 = 6 \text{ ways}$$
> 5. By the multiplication principle:
>    $$\text{Total Valid Rearrangements} = 2 \times 6 = 12$$

</details>

### Question 15
If $j$ and $k$ are even integers and $j < k$, which of the following equals the number of even integers that are greater than $j$ and less than $k$?

- [A] $\dfrac{k - j - 2}{2}$
- [B] $\dfrac{k - j - 1}{2}$
- [C] $\dfrac{k - j}{2}$
- [D] $k - j$
- [E] $k - j - 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[A] $\dfrac{k - j - 2}{2}$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Let $j$ and $k$ be consecutive even numbers first to test the boundary:
>    - Let $j = 4$ and $k = 6$. The even integers strictly between $4$ and $6$ is none (count $= 0$).
>    - Test [A]: $\frac{6 - 4 - 2}{2} = \frac{0}{2} = 0$. (Matches)
>    - Test [C]: $\frac{6 - 4}{2} = 1$. (Fails)
> 2. Test another pair:
>    - Let $j = 4$ and $k = 14$.
>    - The even integers strictly greater than $4$ and less than $14$ are:
>      $$\{6, 8, 10, 12\} \implies 4 \text{ even integers.}$$
>    - Test [A]: $\frac{14 - 4 - 2}{2} = \frac{8}{2} = 4$. (Matches)
> 3. Formal Derivation:
>    - The sequence of even integers between $j$ and $k$ is $j+2, j+4, \dots, k-2$.
>    - This is an arithmetic progression with first term $a_1 = j+2$, last term $a_n = k-2$, and common difference $d = 2$:
>      $$k - 2 = (j + 2) + (n - 1)2$$
>      $$k - j - 4 = 2n - 2 \implies 2n = k - j - 2 \implies n = \frac{k - j - 2}{2}$$

</details>

### Question 17
The interior dimensions of a rectangular tank are as follows: length $110\text{ centimeters}$, width $90\text{ centimeters}$, and height $270\text{ centimeters}$. The tank rests on level ground. Based on the assumption that the volume of water increases by $10$ percent when it freezes, which of the following is closest to the maximum height, in centimeters, to which the tank can be filled with water so that when the water freezes, the ice would not rise above the top of the tank?

- [A] $230$
- [B] $235$
- [C] $240$
- [D] $245$
- [E] $250$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[D] $245$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Let $h$ be the initial height of the liquid water in centimeters.
> 2. The base area of the rectangular tank is $A = 110 \times 90\text{ cm}^2$.
> 3. The initial volume of water is $V_{\text{water}} = A \times h$.
> 4. When water freezes, its volume increases by $10\%$:
>    $$V_{\text{ice}} = 1.10 \times V_{\text{water}} = 1.10 \times (A \times h)$$
> 5. Since the tank has vertical walls with constant base area $A$, the frozen ice rises to height:
>    $$h_{\text{ice}} = \frac{V_{\text{ice}}}{A} = 1.10 h$$
> 6. To prevent the ice from overflowing the top of the tank ($270\text{ cm}$):
>    $$1.10 h \le 270 \implies h \le \frac{270}{1.10} = \frac{2700}{11} \approx 245.45\text{ cm}$$
> 7. The closest choice to $245.45$ is **$245$** ([D]).

</details>

### Question 21
The first term in a certain sequence is $1$, the 2nd term is $2$, and for all $n \ge 3$, the $n$th term in the sequence is the average (arithmetic mean) of the first $n - 1$ terms in the sequence. What is the value of the 6th term in the sequence?

*Give your answer as a fraction.*

$$\frac{\boxed{\phantom{000}}}{\boxed{\phantom{000}}}$$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **$\dfrac{3}{2}$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. We are given:
>    $$a_1 = 1, \quad a_2 = 2$$
> 2. Find $a_3$:
>    $$a_3 = \frac{a_1 + a_2}{2} = \frac{1 + 2}{2} = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5$$
> 3. Find $a_4$:
>    $$a_4 = \frac{a_1 + a_2 + a_3}{3} = \frac{3 + 1.5}{3} = \frac{4.5}{3} = 1.5 = \frac{3}{2}$$
> 4. Mathematical Rule:
>    - In any sequence where each subsequent term $a_n$ is the average of all preceding $n - 1$ terms:
>      $$a_n = \frac{S_{n-1}}{n-1}$$
>    - The new sum becomes $S_n = S_{n-1} + a_n = S_{n-1}\left(\frac{n}{n-1}\right)$.
>    - Then the next term is:
>      $$a_{n+1} = \frac{S_n}{n} = \frac{S_{n-1}}{n-1} = a_n$$
>    - Thus, for all $n \ge 3$, every term is identical and equals $a_3 = \frac{3}{2}$.
> 5. Therefore, the 6th term is $a_6 = \frac{3}{2}$.

</details>
