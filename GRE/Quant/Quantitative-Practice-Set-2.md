### Question 1
$$x < 0$$

| Quantity A | Quantity B |
| :---: | :---: |
| $x^5$ | $x^4$ |

- [A] Quantity A is greater.
- [B] Quantity B is greater.
- [C] The two quantities are equal.
- [D] The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[B] Quantity B is greater.**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. We are given that $x < 0$ (meaning $x$ is a strictly negative real number).
> 2. **Quantity A:** $x^5$ raises a negative base to an odd integer exponent. A negative base raised to an odd power is always strictly negative:
>    $$x^5 < 0$$
> 3. **Quantity B:** $x^4$ raises a negative base to an even integer exponent. Any non-zero real number raised to an even power is always strictly positive:
>    $$x^4 > 0$$
> 4. Since any positive number is strictly greater than any negative number:
>    $$x^5 < 0 < x^4 \implies \text{Quantity B} > \text{Quantity A}$$

</details>

### Question 2
$x$ and $y$ are numbers.

| Quantity A | Quantity B |
| :---: | :---: |
| $(x + 4)(y + 3)$ | $(x + 3)(y + 4)$ |

- [A] Quantity A is greater.
- [B] Quantity B is greater.
- [C] The two quantities are equal.
- [D] The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[D] The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Expand both algebraic expressions:
>    $$\text{Quantity A} = (x + 4)(y + 3) = xy + 3x + 4y + 12$$
>    $$\text{Quantity B} = (x + 3)(y + 4) = xy + 4x + 3y + 12$$
> 2. Perform identical algebraic subtractions from both quantities to simplify the comparison:
>    - Subtract $xy + 12$ from both sides:
>      $$\text{Quantity A} \to 3x + 4y$$
>      $$\text{Quantity B} \to 4x + 3y$$
>    - Subtract $3x + 3y$ from both sides:
>      $$\text{Quantity A} \to y$$
>      $$\text{Quantity B} \to x$$
> 3. The comparison reduces entirely to evaluating $y$ versus $x$.
> 4. Since no relationship between $x$ and $y$ is provided (either can be greater, or they could be equal), the relationship cannot be determined.

</details>

### Question 3
In the notation $0.\bar{b}$, the bar represents a decimal in which the digit $b$ repeats indefinitely.

| Quantity A | Quantity B |
| :---: | :---: |
| $0.\bar{3} + 0.\bar{7}$ | $1.0$ |

- [A] Quantity A is greater.
- [B] Quantity B is greater.
- [C] The two quantities are equal.
- [D] The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[A] Quantity A is greater.**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Convert the repeating decimals into exact fractional representations:
>    $$0.\bar{3} = \frac{3}{9} = \frac{1}{3}$$
>    $$0.\bar{7} = \frac{7}{9}$$
> 2. Calculate the sum for **Quantity A**:
>    $$\text{Quantity A} = \frac{3}{9} + \frac{7}{9} = \frac{10}{9} = 1.\overline{1} \approx 1.111...$$
> 3. Compare with **Quantity B**:
>    $$\text{Quantity B} = 1.0 = \frac{9}{9}$$
> 4. Since $\frac{10}{9} > \frac{9}{9}$, **Quantity A is greater**.

</details>

### Question 4
A company plans to manufacture two types of hammers, type $R$ and type $S$.  
The cost of manufacturing each hammer of type $S$ is $\$0.05$ less than twice the cost of manufacturing each hammer of type $R$.

| Quantity A | Quantity B |
| :---: | :---: |
| The cost of manufacturing $1,000$ hammers of type $R$ and $1,000$ hammers of type $S$ | The cost of manufacturing $1,500$ hammers of type $S$ |

- [A] Quantity A is greater.
- [B] Quantity B is greater.
- [C] The two quantities are equal.
- [D] The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[A] Quantity A is greater.**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Let $r$ denote the manufacturing cost of a type $R$ hammer in dollars.
> 2. The manufacturing cost $s$ of a type $S$ hammer is:
>    $$s = 2r - 0.05$$
> 3. Express both quantities in terms of $r$:
>    $$\begin{aligned}
>    \text{Quantity A} &= 1000r + 1000s = 1000r + 1000(2r - 0.05) \\
>    &= 1000r + 2000r - 50 = 3000r - 50
>    \end{aligned}$$
>    $$\begin{aligned}
>    \text{Quantity B} &= 1500s = 1500(2r - 0.05) \\
>    &= 3000r - 75
>    \end{aligned}$$
> 4. Compare Quantity A and Quantity B:
>    $$\text{Quantity A} - \text{Quantity B} = (3000r - 50) - (3000r - 75) = -50 + 75 = 25 > 0$$
> 5. For any positive production cost $r > 0$, Quantity A is always $\$25$ greater than Quantity B. Therefore, **Quantity A is greater**.

</details>

### Question 6
$$\text{List } X: 2, 5, s, t \qquad \text{List } Y: 2, 5, t$$
The average (arithmetic mean) of the numbers in list $X$ is equal to the average of the numbers in list $Y$.

| Quantity A | Quantity B |
| :---: | :---: |
| $s$ | $0$ |

- [A] Quantity A is greater.
- [B] Quantity B is greater.
- [C] The two quantities are equal.
- [D] The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[D] The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Equate the arithmetic means of the two lists:
>    $$\frac{2 + 5 + s + t}{4} = \frac{2 + 5 + t}{3}$$
>    $$\frac{7 + s + t}{4} = \frac{7 + t}{3}$$
> 2. Cross-multiply to solve for $s$ in terms of $t$:
>    $$3(7 + s + t) = 4(7 + t)$$
>    $$21 + 3s + 3t = 28 + 4t$$
>    $$3s = 7 + t \implies s = \frac{7 + t}{3}$$
> 3. Test values for $t$:
>    - If $t = -7$, then $s = \frac{7 - 7}{3} = 0$. Here, $\text{Quantity A} = \text{Quantity B}$.
>    - If $t = 2$, then $s = \frac{7 + 2}{3} = 3 > 0$. Here, $\text{Quantity A} > \text{Quantity B}$.
>    - If $t = -13$, then $s = \frac{7 - 13}{3} = -2 < 0$. Here, $\text{Quantity A} < \text{Quantity B}$.
> 4. Since the sign of $s$ depends entirely on the unspecified value of $t$, the relationship cannot be determined.

</details>

### Question 7
The radius of circle $A$ is $12$ inches greater than the radius of circle $B$.

| Quantity A | Quantity B |
| :---: | :---: |
| The circumference of circle $A$ minus the circumference of circle $B$ | $72$ |

- [A] Quantity A is greater.
- [B] Quantity B is greater.
- [C] The two quantities are equal.
- [D] The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[A] Quantity A is greater.**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Let $r_B$ be the radius of circle $B$. The radius of circle $A$ is:
>    $$r_A = r_B + 12$$
> 2. The formula for the circumference of a circle is $C = 2\pi r$.
> 3. Calculate the difference in circumferences (**Quantity A**):
>    $$\Delta C = 2\pi r_A - 2\pi r_B = 2\pi (r_A - r_B) = 2\pi(12) = 24\pi$$
> 4. Approximate $24\pi$ using $\pi \approx 3.14159$:
>    $$24\pi \approx 24 \times 3.14159 = 75.398$$
> 5. Compare with **Quantity B**:
>    $$75.398 > 72 \implies \text{Quantity A is greater.}$$

</details>

### Question 8
In triangle $ABC$, $AB = BC$ and the measure of angle $B$ is $40^\circ$.  
The measure of angle $A$ is $x^\circ$.

| Quantity A | Quantity B |
| :---: | :---: |
| $x$ | $120$ |

- [A] Quantity A is greater.
- [B] Quantity B is greater.
- [C] The two quantities are equal.
- [D] The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[B] Quantity B is greater.**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Triangle $ABC$ is an isosceles triangle with sides $AB = BC$.
> 2. By the Isosceles Triangle Theorem, the angles opposite to these sides are equal:
>    $$\angle A = \angle C = x^\circ$$
> 3. The sum of interior angles in any Euclidean triangle is $180^\circ$:
>    $$\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180^\circ$$
>    $$x + 40 + x = 180$$
>    $$2x = 140 \implies x = 70$$
> 4. Compare quantities:
>    $$\text{Quantity A} = 70, \quad \text{Quantity B} = 120$$
> 5. Since $70 < 120$, **Quantity B is greater**.

</details>

### Question 9
Set $S = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8\}$.

| Quantity A | Quantity B |
| :---: | :---: |
| The number of 4-member subsets of $S$ | The number of 5-member subsets of $S$ |

- [A] Quantity A is greater.
- [B] Quantity B is greater.
- [C] The two quantities are equal.
- [D] The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[A] Quantity A is greater.**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Set $S$ contains $n = 8$ distinct elements.
> 2. The number of $k$-element subsets chosen from an $n$-element set is given by the combination formula $\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}$.
> 3. **Quantity A:**
>    $$\binom{8}{4} = \frac{8 \times 7 \times 6 \times 5}{4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} = \frac{1680}{24} = 70$$
> 4. **Quantity B:**
>    $$\binom{8}{5} = \binom{8}{3} = \frac{8 \times 7 \times 6}{3 \times 2 \times 1} = 56$$
> 5. Comparing quantities: $70 > 56$, so **Quantity A is greater**.

</details>

### Question 10
In right triangle $ABC$, the ratio of the lengths of the two legs is $2$ to $5$. If the area of triangle $ABC$ is $20$, what is the length of the hypotenuse?

- [A] $2\sqrt{5}$
- [B] $10$
- [C] $\sqrt{58}$
- [D] $29$
- [E] $2\sqrt{29}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[E] $2\sqrt{29}$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Let the lengths of the two perpendicular legs be $2k$ and $5k$, where $k > 0$.
> 2. The area of a right triangle is given by $\frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$:
>    $$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(2k)(5k) = 5k^2$$
> 3. Set the area equal to $20$:
>    $$5k^2 = 20 \implies k^2 = 4 \implies k = 2$$
> 4. Find the actual lengths of the two legs:
>    $$\text{Leg}_1 = 2(2) = 4, \quad \text{Leg}_2 = 5(2) = 10$$
> 5. Use the Pythagorean theorem to calculate the hypotenuse $c$:
>    $$c = \sqrt{4^2 + 10^2} = \sqrt{16 + 100} = \sqrt{116}$$
> 6. Simplify the radical:
>    $$\sqrt{116} = \sqrt{4 \times 29} = 2\sqrt{29}$$

</details>

### Question 11
According to surveys at a company, $20$ percent of the employees owned cell phones in 1994 and $60$ percent of the employees owned cell phones in 1998. From 1994 to 1998, what was the percent increase in the fraction of employees who owned cell phones?

- [A] $3\%$
- [B] $40\%$
- [C] $66\frac{2}{3}\%$
- [D] $200\%$
- [E] $300\%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[D] $200\%$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. The initial value in 1994 was $20\%$ (or $0.20$).
> 2. The new value in 1998 was $60\%$ (or $0.60$).
> 3. The formula for percent increase is:
>    $$\text{Percent Increase} = \frac{\text{New Value} - \text{Old Value}}{\text{Old Value}} \times 100\%$$
> 4. Substitute the values:
>    $$\text{Percent Increase} = \frac{60 - 20}{20} \times 100\% = \frac{40}{20} \times 100\% = 2 \times 100\% = 200\%$$
> *(Note: A common trap is simply subtracting $60\% - 20\% = 40\%$, which is the percentage point change, not the percentage increase).*

</details>

### Question 12
If $t$ is an integer and $8m = 16^t$, which of the following expresses $m$ in terms of $t$?

- [A] $2^{t-3}$
- [B] $2^{2t-3}$
- [C] $2^{3t-3}$
- [D] $2^{4t-3}$
- [E] $2^{4t-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[D] $2^{4t-3}$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Convert all numbers to common base $2$:
>    $$8 = 2^3$$
>    $$16 = 2^4 \implies 16^t = (2^4)^t = 2^{4t}$$
> 2. Substitute into the given equation:
>    $$2^3 \cdot m = 2^{4t}$$
> 3. Solve for $m$ by dividing both sides by $2^3$:
>    $$m = \frac{2^{4t}}{2^3} = 2^{4t - 3}$$

</details>

### Question 13
Three pumps, $P$, $R$, and $T$, working simultaneously at their respective constant rates, can fill a tank in $5$ hours. Pumps $P$ and $R$, working simultaneously at their respective constant rates, can fill the tank in $7$ hours. How many hours will it take pump $T$, working alone at its constant rate, to fill the tank?

- [A] $7.0$
- [B] $12.0$
- [C] $15.0$
- [D] $17.5$
- [E] $30.0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[D] $17.5$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Express work rates in tanks filled per hour:
>    $$\text{Combined Rate}(P + R + T) = \frac{1}{5} \text{ tanks/hour}$$
>    $$\text{Combined Rate}(P + R) = \frac{1}{7} \text{ tanks/hour}$$
> 2. Subtract the rate of $(P + R)$ from $(P + R + T)$ to isolate the rate of pump $T$:
>    $$\text{Rate}(T) = \frac{1}{5} - \frac{1}{7} = \frac{7 - 5}{35} = \frac{2}{35} \text{ tanks/hour}$$
> 3. The time required for pump $T$ to fill $1$ full tank alone is the reciprocal of its rate:
>    $$\text{Time} = \frac{1}{\text{Rate}(T)} = \frac{35}{2} = 17.5 \text{ hours}$$

</details>

### Question 14
The perimeter of a flat rectangular lawn is $42$ meters. The width of the lawn is $75$ percent of its length. What is the area of the lawn, in square meters?

- [A] $40.5$
- [B] $96$
- [C] $108$
- [D] $192$
- [E] $432$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[C] $108$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Let $l$ be the length of the lawn. The width $w$ is $75\%$ of the length:
>    $$w = 0.75l = \frac{3}{4}l$$
> 2. The perimeter of a rectangle is $2(l + w) = 42$:
>    $$2\left(l + \frac{3}{4}l\right) = 42$$
>    $$2\left(\frac{7}{4}l\right) = 42 \implies \frac{7}{2}l = 42$$
>    $$l = 42 \times \frac{2}{7} = 12 \text{ meters}$$
> 3. Calculate the width:
>    $$w = \frac{3}{4}(12) = 9 \text{ meters}$$
> 4. Calculate the area:
>    $$\text{Area} = l \times w = 12 \times 9 = 108 \text{ square meters}$$

</details>

### Question 15
The greatest of the $21$ positive integers in a certain list is $16$. The median of the $21$ integers is $10$. What is the least possible average (arithmetic mean) of the $21$ integers?

- [A] $4$
- [B] $5$
- [C] $6$
- [D] $7$
- [E] $8$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[C] $6$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Let the $21$ positive integers arranged in non-decreasing order be:
>    $$x_1 \le x_2 \le \dots \le x_{10} \le x_{11} \le x_{12} \le \dots \le x_{20} \le x_{21}$$
> 2. For an ordered set of $21$ integers, the median is the 11th term:
>    $$x_{11} = 10$$
> 3. The maximum term is $x_{21} = 16$.
> 4. To minimize the arithmetic mean, we must minimize the total sum $\sum_{i=1}^{21} x_i$:
>    - Because the integers must be positive integers, the minimum possible value for $x_1$ through $x_{10}$ is $1$:
>      $$x_1 = x_2 = \dots = x_{10} = 1 \implies \text{Sum} = 10 \times 1 = 10$$
>    - The 11th integer is fixed at the median: $x_{11} = 10$.
>    - For the terms above the median ($x_{12}$ through $x_{20}$), each must satisfy $x_i \ge x_{11} = 10$. To minimize the sum, set each to the smallest allowed value, $10$:
>      $$x_{12} = x_{13} = \dots = x_{20} = 10 \implies \text{Sum} = 9 \times 10 = 90$$
>    - The 21st integer is $x_{21} = 16$.
> 5. Compute the minimum total sum:
>    $$\text{Min Sum} = 10(1) + 10 + 9(10) + 16 = 10 + 10 + 90 + 16 = 126$$
> 6. Compute the minimum average:
>    $$\text{Min Average} = \frac{126}{21} = 6$$

</details>

### Question 16
If $x$ and $y$ are positive integers and:
$$x = \frac{(2)(3)(4)(5)(7)(11)(13)}{39y}$$
Which of the following could be the value of $y$?

- [A] $15$
- [B] $28$
- [C] $38$
- [D] $64$
- [E] $143$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[B] $28$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Factor the denominator: $39y = 3 \times 13 \times y$.
> 2. Cancel common prime factors $3$ and $13$ from the numerator and denominator:
>    $$x = \frac{(2)(3)(4)(5)(7)(11)(13)}{3 \times 13 \times y} = \frac{2 \times 4 \times 5 \times 7 \times 11}{y}$$
> 3. Express the remaining numerator in prime factorization form:
>    $$\text{Numerator} = 2^1 \times 2^2 \times 5 \times 7 \times 11 = 2^3 \times 5^1 \times 7^1 \times 11^1$$
> 4. Since $x$ is an integer, $y$ must divide $2^3 \times 5 \times 7 \times 11$ without leaving any remainder:
>    - **[A] $15 = 3 \times 5$:** Contains factor $3$, which is not in the numerator.
>    - **[B] $28 = 4 \times 7 = 2^2 \times 7$:** Both $2^2$ and $7$ divide $2^3 \times 7$. Thus, $y = 28$ produces an integer ($x = 2 \times 5 \times 11 = 110$).
>    - **[C] $38 = 2 \times 19$:** Contains prime $19$.
>    - **[D] $64 = 2^6$:** Requires six factors of $2$, but the numerator only has $2^3$.
>    - **[E] $143 = 11 \times 13$:** Contains prime $13$.
> 5. Hence, only $y = 28$ is possible.

</details>

### Question 17
Of the $40$ specimens of bacteria in a dish, $3$ specimens have a certain trait. If $5$ specimens are to be selected from the dish at random and without replacement, which of the following expressions represents the probability that only $1$ of the $5$ specimens selected will have the trait?

- [A] $\dfrac{3}{40}$
- [B] $\dfrac{\binom{3}{1}}{\binom{40}{5}}$
- [C] $\dfrac{\binom{3}{1}\binom{37}{5}}{\binom{40}{5}}$
- [D] $\dfrac{\binom{3}{1}\binom{40}{4}}{\binom{40}{5}}$
- [E] $\dfrac{\binom{3}{1}\binom{37}{4}}{\binom{40}{5}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[E] $\dfrac{\binom{3}{1}\binom{37}{4}}{\binom{40}{5}}$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Total specimens in the dish: $40$.
>    - Specimens with the trait: $3$.
>    - Specimens without the trait: $40 - 3 = 37$.
> 2. Total ways to select $5$ specimens from $40$ without replacement:
>    $$\text{Total Outcomes} = \binom{40}{5}$$
> 3. Favorable outcomes where **exactly $1$** specimen has the trait:
>    - Select $1$ specimen from the $3$ that have the trait: $\binom{3}{1}$ ways.
>    - Select the remaining $4$ specimens from the $37$ that do not have the trait: $\binom{37}{4}$ ways.
> 4. By the Fundamental Counting Principle:
>    $$\text{Favorable Outcomes} = \binom{3}{1} \times \binom{37}{4}$$
> 5. Therefore, the probability is:
>    $$P = \frac{\binom{3}{1}\binom{37}{4}}{\binom{40}{5}}$$

</details>

## Multiple-Choice: Select One or More (Questions 18–19)

### Question 18
Two different positive integers $x$ and $y$ are selected from the odd integers that are less than $10$. If $z = x + y$ and $z$ is less than $10$, which of the following integers could be the sum of $x$, $y$, and $z$?  
*Indicate all such integers.*

- [A] $8$
- [B] $10$
- [C] $11$
- [D] $12$
- [E] $14$
- [F] $15$
- [G] $16$
- [H] $18$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answers:** **[A] $8$, [D] $12$, [G] $16$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. The odd positive integers less than $10$ are $\{1, 3, 5, 7, 9\}$.
> 2. We select two distinct integers $x < y$. Then $z = x + y$.
> 3. The desired quantity is $x + y + z = (x + y) + z = z + z = 2z$.
> 4. We are given the condition that $z < 10$:
>    - If $\{x, y\} = \{1, 3\} \implies z = 1 + 3 = 4 < 10$. Sum $= 2(4) = 8$.
>    - If $\{x, y\} = \{1, 5\} \implies z = 1 + 5 = 6 < 10$. Sum $= 2(6) = 12$.
>    - If $\{x, y\} = \{1, 7\} \implies z = 1 + 7 = 8 < 10$. Sum $= 2(8) = 16$.
>    - If $\{x, y\} = \{3, 5\} \implies z = 3 + 5 = 8 < 10$. Sum $= 2(8) = 16$.
>    - All other pairs have $x + y \ge 10$ (e.g., $1+9=10$, $3+7=10$, $5+7=12$), violating $z < 10$.
> 5. The only possible values of $x + y + z$ are $8$, $12$, and $16$.

</details>

### Question 19
For a certain probability experiment, the probability that event $A$ will occur is $\frac{1}{2}$ and the probability that event $B$ will occur is $\frac{1}{3}$. Which of the following values could be the probability that the event $A \cup B$ (that is, the event $A$ or $B$, or both) will occur?  
*Indicate all such values.*

- [A] $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- [B] $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- [C] $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- [D] $\dfrac{7}{8}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answers:** **[B] $\dfrac{1}{2}$, [C] $\dfrac{3}{4}$**
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. By the general addition rule of probability:
>    $$P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)$$
> 2. Given $P(A) = \frac{1}{2}$ and $P(B) = \frac{1}{3}$:
>    $$P(A \cup B) = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} - P(A \cap B) = \frac{5}{6} - P(A \cap B)$$
> 3. Determine the possible bounds for $P(A \cap B)$:
>    - **Minimum intersection:** If $A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive, $P(A \cap B) = 0$.
>      $$P(A \cup B) = \frac{5}{6} \approx 0.833$$
>    - **Maximum intersection:** When event $B$ is completely contained within event $A$, $P(A \cap B) = P(B) = \frac{1}{3}$.
>      $$P(A \cup B) = \frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{2} = 0.50$$
> 4. Thus, $P(A \cup B)$ must satisfy:
>    $$\frac{1}{2} \le P(A \cup B) \le \frac{5}{6} \quad (0.50 \le P(A \cup B) \le 0.833...)$$
> 5. Test each option:
>    - **[A] $\frac{1}{3} \approx 0.333 < 0.50$:** Impossible.
>    - **[B] $\frac{1}{2} = 0.50$:** Possible (at minimum bound).
>    - **[C] $\frac{3}{4} = 0.75$:** Lies inside $[0.50, 0.833]$, possible.
>    - **[D] $\frac{7}{8} = 0.875 > 0.833$:** Exceeds maximum bound, impossible.

</details>

### Question 20
If $a$ and $b$ are the two solutions of the equation $x^2 - 5x + 4 = 0$, what is the value of:
$$\left(\frac{1 + a}{a}\right)\left(\frac{1 + b}{b}\right)$$

*Give your answer as a fraction.*

$$\frac{\boxed{\phantom{000}}}{\boxed{\phantom{000}}}$$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **$\dfrac{5}{2}$** (or equivalent unreduced fraction such as $\frac{10}{4}$)
>
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> **Method 1: Find the explicit roots**
> 1. Factor the quadratic equation:
>    $$x^2 - 5x + 4 = 0 \implies (x - 1)(x - 4) = 0$$
> 2. The roots are $a = 1$ and $b = 4$ (or vice versa).
> 3. Substitute into the expression:
>    $$\left(\frac{1 + 1}{1}\right)\left(\frac{1 + 4}{4}\right) = (2)\left(\frac{5}{4}\right) = \frac{10}{4} = \frac{5}{2}$$
>
> **Method 2: Using Vieta's Formulas**
> 1. For $x^2 - 5x + 4 = 0$, the sum of roots is $a + b = 5$ and product of roots is $ab = 4$.
> 2. Expand the expression:
>    $$\left(1 + \frac{1}{a}\right)\left(1 + \frac{1}{b}\right) = 1 + \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{ab} = 1 + \frac{a+b}{ab} + \frac{1}{ab}$$
> 3. Substitute $a+b = 5$ and $ab = 4$:
>    $$1 + \frac{5}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = 1 + \frac{6}{4} = 1 + \frac{3}{2} = \frac{5}{2}$$

</details>
