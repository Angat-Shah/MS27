# GRE Verbal Reasoning: Sentence Equivalence Practice

Sentence Equivalence (SE) questions consist of a single sentence with one blank and six answer choices. You must select the **two** choices that lead to a complete, coherent sentence and produce completed sentences that are **substantially alike in meaning**.

### Key Rules & Strategies
1. **Always select two choices.** There is no partial credit on the GRE.
2. **Find the clue and direction:** Look for transition words (*although, however, because, despite, furthermore*) that establish contrast or agreement with the clue.
3. **Pre-think your own word:** Formulate your own synonym before looking at the choices to avoid being tricked by traps.
4. **Beware of synonym pairs that don't fit the sentence:** Two words can be perfect synonyms, but if they don't match the sentence's contextual meaning, they are incorrect.
5. **Beware of single words that fit:** An option might make grammatical and logical sense on its own, but if it has no companion choice producing equivalent meaning, it cannot be part of the correct answer.

### Question 1
As my eyesight began to ________, I spent a lot of time writing about it—both poems and essays—describing what I saw as I looked out through damaged eyes.

- [A] deteriorate
- [B] sharpen
- [C] improve
- [D] decline
- [E] recover
- [F] adjust

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[A] deteriorate** and **[D] decline**
>
> **Explanation:**  
> The clue later in the sentence mentions looking out through "damaged eyes." This indicates that the author's vision was worsening over time. Both **deteriorate** and **decline** mean to degenerate or become progressively worse.

> *Incorrect choices:* **sharpen**, **improve**, and **recover** imply positive visual development, which directly contradicts "damaged eyes." **Adjust** means to adapt, which does not convey deterioration and lacks an equivalent partner choice.

### Question 2
The judge's standing in the legal community, though shaken by phony allegations of wrongdoing, emerged, at long last, ________.

- [A] unqualified
- [B] undiminished
- [C] undecided
- [D] undamaged
- [E] unresolved
- [F] unprincipled

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[B] undiminished** and **[D] undamaged**
>
> **Explanation:**  
> The word "though" signals a contrast between the initial threat ("shaken by phony allegations") and the eventual outcome ("at long last"). Despite the allegations, the judge's reputation survived without loss of esteem. **Undiminished** (not reduced in stature or degree) and **undamaged** (unharmed; intact) create sentences with identical meaning.  
> 
> *Incorrect choices:* **unqualified** (absolute, or lacking qualifications), **undecided** (unsettled), **unresolved** (not determined), and **unprincipled** (lacking moral scruples) do not logically complete the sentence or provide a parallel pair.
