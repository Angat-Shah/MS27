# GRE Verbal Reasoning: Reading Comprehension Practice

Reading Comprehension (RC) questions evaluate your ability to read with a deep understanding of words and sentences, summarize arguments, distinguish main ideas from supporting details, analyze text structure, draw valid inferences, and evaluate reasoning.

### Question Formats on the GRE
1. **Multiple-Choice (Select One):** Traditional 5-choice questions where exactly one answer choice is correct.
2. **Multiple-Choice (Select One or More):** Three answer choices (labeled [A], [B], [C]). You must select all choices that apply (one, two, or all three). There is no partial credit.
3. **Select-in-Passage:** Clicking or identifying a specific sentence in the passage that fits a given functional description.
4. **Critical Reasoning (Argument Analysis):** Short 1-paragraph arguments testing logic (assumptions, strengthening, weakening, paradox resolution).

### Passage 1 (Questions 1-2)
Ragwort was accidentally introduced to New Zealand in the late nineteenth century and, like so many invading foreign species, quickly became a pest. By the 1920s, the weed was rampant. What made matters worse was that its proliferation coincided with sweeping changes in agriculture and a massive shift from sheep farming to dairying. Ragwort contains a battery of toxic and resilient alkaloids: even honey made from its flowers contains the poison in dilute form. Livestock generally avoid grazing where ragwort is growing, but they will do so once it displaces grass and clover in their pasture. Though sheep can eat it for months before showing any signs of illness, if cattle eat it they sicken quickly, and fatality can even result.

- [B] took place in conditions that enabled the ragwort to spread faster than it otherwise would have done

- [D] prevented people from producing honey that could be eaten safely
- [E] had consequences for livestock that were more dramatic than they otherwise would have been

> **Correct Answer:** **[E] had consequences for livestock that were more dramatic than they otherwise would have been**
>
> **Explanation:**  
> The passage explicitly explains what made the proliferation worse: it coincided with a massive shift from sheep farming to dairy cattle farming. Because cattle sicken quickly and die from eating ragwort whereas sheep can tolerate it for months, the timing made the negative impact on livestock far more severe than it would have been under sheep farming.

Ultra-high-energy cosmic rays (UHECRs)—subatomic particles possessing kinetic energies exceeding $10^{19}$ electron volts—have long posed a profound puzzle for astrophysicists. Consequently, any UHECR detected on Earth must originate from relatively nearby extragalactic sources. Observations from the Pierre Auger Observatory indicate that the arrival directions of the highest-energy rays correlate with the positions of nearby active galactic nuclei (AGN), supermassive black holes expelling relativistic jets of plasma. However, the correlation is far from perfect: some detected UHECRs point toward celestial regions devoid of nearby AGNs, while several prominent nearby AGNs have yielded no corresponding particle detections. <summary><b>View Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

> **Correct Answer:** **[A], [B], and [C]**

> **Explanation:**
