## Essay

Leadership requires much more than individual talent; it demands the ability to work effectively with others. Although competition can encourage people to improve their performance, successful leaders must inspire teamwork, resolve conflicts, and achieve common goals. Admittedly, society should emphasize cooperation over competition when preparing young people for leadership roles.

To begin with, cooperation develops qualities that are essential for effective leadership. For instance, the success of the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), particularly for the Chandrayaan-3 mission, was possible because thousands of scientists and engineers collaborated together to achieve a common goal. No single individual could have accomplished such a complex mission. Similarly, leaders in government must work with different departments, experts, and citizens to solve national problems. These examples demonstrate that cooperation builds communication, trust, and collective decision-making, all of which are indispensable for leadership.

Nevertheless, some may argue that competition is a better teacher than cooperation because it motivates individuals to perform extraordinarily, become innovative and strive for excellence. For instance, The Indian Civil Service Examination is highly competitive which prompts candidates to develop strong knowledge, discipline, and perseverance. Similarly, competition among businesses often leads to technological innovation and better service for consumers. However, competition alone cannot make you capable of leadership positions. Once individuals become administrators, ministers, or cooperative executives, their success depends on collaborating with colleagues, managing diverse teams, and building consensus. Thus, competition may allow individuals to reach leadership positions, but cooperation is the key for their long-term success in those careers.

Taken together, while healthy competition is necessary for people to boost their knowledge, skills, and innovative mindset, cooperation is ultimately the most important quality for leadership. It prepares individuals to unite people, solve complex problems, and achieve lasting results. Therefore, societies should place greater emphasis on fostering cooperation among young people while allowing competition to serve as a second source of motivation.
