# AWA M01 – Medical Transparency and Patient Choice

**“An ailing patient should have easy access to his or her doctor’s record of treating similarly afflicted patients. Through gaining such access, the ailing patient may better determine whether the doctor is competent to treat that medical condition.”**

In developing and supporting your position, describe specific circumstances in which adopting the recommendation would or would not be advantageous and explain how these examples shape your position.

## Essay

Patients suffering from serious medical issues should be given necessary and important information so that they can make an informed decision about their treatment. According to people who argue that patients should be given access to a doctor's previous operation records, this is considerably appropriate. However, this access should be carefully designed because, in an attempt to ease the confusion of one patient, it will in turn expose the confidential information of another patient whose record will be shown.

The most compelling argument for this claim is that information about a doctor's previous record of examination enables patients to make informed choices. Several medical procedures involve risks, and patients generally want to know about the person who will be operating on them, to see if they have succeeded previously in similar cases. For example, a person who is suffering from severe cancer or a heart attack would gain more confidence in choosing a surgeon who has handled several similar cases and has succeeded. Access to such information allows patients to choose freely instead of being forcibly decided solely on the basis of reputation or advertising.

Nevertheless, unrestricted access to medical records could create unethical problems. Patient records contain several confidential pieces of information that they prefer to keep private. Revealing previous patient information could violate patient confidentiality and undermine trust in the healthcare system. Instead, a balanced approach could be followed to achieve the same result while not breaching the boundary of one's private information, which would be to allow patients to view the physician's qualifications, experience, board certifications, and treatment outcomes. This process could safely preserve the confidentiality of one patient and also provide essential evidence to another for evaluating medical competence.

Overall, patients should have a reasonable amount of access to information about their doctor's experience regarding similar medical situations to aid in the process of making informed decisions about medical procedures. However, this transparency should be carefully implemented, with a well-designed solution that can strongly safeguard the medical information and history of former patients and also provide essential evidence to current ones.
