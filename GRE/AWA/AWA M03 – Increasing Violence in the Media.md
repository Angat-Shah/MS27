# AWA M03 – Increasing Violence in the Media

> Write a response in which you discuss which view more closely aligns with your own position and explain your reasoning for the position you take. In developing and supporting your position, you should address both of the views presented above.

## Essay

Violence in modern media has increased through movies, television, social media, and video games. Many people believe that this exposure encourages children to behave aggressively. Others argue that parents and friends have a much greater influence on how children’s behavior develops. Although violent media can affect children’s thoughts and emotions, I believe that parental guidance and peer groups play a more important role in shaping children’s behavior because they influence children on a regular basis.

Supporters of the first view argue that children often imitate what they watch. Young children may not always understand the difference between a virtual environment and reality. For example, the massive popularity of action-packed FPS (first-person shooter) video games like BGMI (formerly PUBG) exposes children to stylized and normalized violence. When young fans see their favourite cinematic heroes using aggression to solve every problem they face, they tend to imitate their behavior, leading to playground fights or the use of disrespectful language. Therefore, the media should not be ignored as a possible influence on children’s behavior.

However, parents and friends have a stronger and more lasting impact because they are directly involved in a child’s life. Parents teach children values such as kindness, honesty, and self-control from an early age. A child raised in a family or society where kindness, discipline, and caring behavior are encouraged is less likely to become violent, even if they watch violent media. Similarly, peer groups play the same role in influencing children’s decisions. For instance, a student who spends time with classmates who respect rules and help others is more likely to develop positive habits, whereas a child surrounded by aggressive and abusive friends may eventually adopt similar behavior. This explains why children living in the same society and watching similar media often behave very differently.

Overall, while violent media can contribute to children’s aggressive behavior, it is not the main cause of violence. Parents and peer groups shape children’s attitudes, values, and daily choices more directly than the media. For this reason, I believe that strong family support and positive friendships are the most effective ways to reduce aggression among children.
