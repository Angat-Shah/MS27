## Essay

Celebrities, particularly movie and sports stars, enjoy immense popularity and influence in modern society. Millions of young people admire them and often imitate their behavior, appearance, and attitudes. Because of this massive reach, these public figures do have a moral obligation to serve as positive role models. However, expecting them to be morally perfect at all times is unrealistic.

The most compelling argument in favor of this obligation is that celebrities significantly shape young people's beliefs and behavior. Children and teenagers often admire successful athletes and actors even more than their teachers or political leaders. Consequently, the actions of these stars can influence the values of an entire generation. For instance, Virat Kohli, the renowned cricketer, is one of the most followed athletes in India. When he demonstrates discipline, dedication, and a healthy lifestyle, young fans naturally try to inculcate these qualities into their own lives. When he publicly shifted to a vegan diet, it made a positive, widespread impact on the youth by promoting healthier life choices.

Nevertheless, it is unfair to demand absolute moral perfection from celebrities at all times, as they are ordinary human beings who will inevitably make mistakes. For instance, Shah Rukh Khan, one of India's biggest movie superstars, appeared in an ad campaign for the tobacco brand "Vimal". From his perspective, he was likely just doing his job as an actor and earning a living. However, the impact on the youth was undeniably negative, showing how easily their influence can lead young fans astray. While stars should try to acknowledge these missteps, society must also recognize the limits of a celebrity's duty. Parents, teachers, and educational institutions bear the primary responsibility for teaching moral values, and we cannot rely solely on entertainers or athletes to raise children.

Ultimately, while sports and movie stars deserve their privacy and should not be treated as flawless individuals, they do carry a moral obligation to behave responsibly. Their immense influence, combined with their public recognition, makes it entirely reasonable for society to expect them to try their best to set a positive example, even if they sometimes fall short.
