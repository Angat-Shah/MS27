# AWA M05 – Talent and Popularity of Musicians

**“The real talent of a popular musician cannot accurately be assessed until the musician has been dead for several generations, so that his or her fame does not interfere with honest assessment.”**
> Write a response in which you discuss the extent to which you agree or disagree with the claim. In developing and supporting your position, be sure to address the most compelling reasons and/or examples that could be used to challenge your position.

## Essay

The statement argues that the true talent of a popular musician cannot be judged until several generations have passed because contemporary fame prevents an honest evaluation. While time certainly separates lasting artistic value from temporary popularity, it is unreasonable to claim that accurate assessment is impossible during a musician's lifetime. A fair approach to evaluation should be to consider both the opinions of contemporary experts and the test of time.

A primary consideration is that many musicians receive recognition not merely because of their popularity but because of their exceptional skill and innovation. In India, A. R. Rahman has earned acclaim through his originality, versatility, and technical excellence rather than through fame alone. His Academy Awards, Grammy Awards, and enduring influence on Indian and international music demonstrate that informed critics and audiences can recognize genuine talent even while an artist is actively creating music. Similarly, Arijit Singh is regarded as one of the finest musicians India has, not merely because of his popularity or celebrity status, but because of his amazing voice, writing skills, and melodies. Moreover, his contributions extend beyond singing, as he possesses considerable expertise in music composition, arrangement, and production. These examples demonstrate that a musician's talent can be recognized and evaluated objectively during his or her lifetime, despite widespread popularity.

At the same time, the statement contains an element of truth because immediate popularity can sometimes exaggerate a musician's reputation. Trends driven by social media, aggressive marketing, or celebrity culture may temporarily elevate artists whose work later loses relevance. Conversely, some musicians receive greater appreciation only after their deaths. Nevertheless, waiting for several generations is an excessively strict standard. Musicologists, critics, and experienced performers can evaluate aspects such as vocal ability, compositional complexity, and innovation using objective criteria. Moreover, the continued popularity of musicians such as Lata Mangeshkar across multiple decades already demonstrates enduring artistic excellence without requiring centuries of historical distance.

Taken together, the passage of time is undoubtedly useful in determining whether a musician's work possesses lasting value, but it is not the sole measure of artistic talent. Contemporary evaluations can provide a reliable assessment during a musician's lifetime. The most balanced judgment emerges from combining present-day critical evaluation with historical perspective, rather than postponing meaningful assessment for several generations.
