## Essay

Every society is shaped by a wide range of economic, cultural, and social forces. Because major cities serve as the centers of government, commerce, and innovations, studying them would reveal many important aspects of a nation. However, to extrapolate a society's true character, studying solely its major cities would not suffice. While metropolises provide valuable insights, it is the smaller towns and rural communities that preserve its foundational tradition, dialects, values, and lifestyle.

Major cities often reflect a nation's modernized and globalized character rather than its complete cultural identity. In India, metropolitan cities such as Mumbai, Delhi, and Bengaluru are dominated by multinational corporations, technological innovation, and professional environments where English is widely used. However, this urban picture does not represent the cultural diversity found across the country. For instance, in Tamil Nadu, the Tamil language is not only used in daily conversation but also in classical literature, cinema, and education, preserving ancient literary tradition. Similarly, food habits also differ significantly — while urban areas may favor global cuisine like burgers, pizzas, and pastas, rural regions in states like Punjab continue to rely on traditional diets such as 'makki di roti' and 'sarson da saag', which are closely tied to agricultural lifestyles. These examples show that cultural identity in Indian society is far more layered and cannot be fully understood by focusing only on metropolitan life.

Furthermore, the urban-rural gap is also evident in the United States, particularly when comparing cities like New York with smaller towns and suburban or countryside areas. In New York, one can observe a highly dense environment with tall residential buildings, crowded streets, and heavy use of public transport such as subways and taxis. However, in smaller towns and other regions away from major cities, the physical and social environment is noticeably different. Houses are generally more spaced out, and most people rely on their private cars rather than public transport. This contrast shows that urban life represents only one part of American society, rather than its overall reality.

In sum, major cities are undoubtedly important as they offer valuable information about a society's economic and cultural dynamics, but they are only one side of a coin. To comprehend a nation fully, one must also examine the smaller communities. Only by integrating both urban and non-urban perspectives can a researcher develop a balanced and authentic understanding of a society.
