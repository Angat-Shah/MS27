# AWA 07 – Science and Religion

**“Science is meaningless without religion.”**
> Write a response in which you discuss the extent to which you agree or disagree with the statement and explain your reasoning for the position you take. In developing and supporting your position, you should consider ways in which the statement might or might not hold true and explain how these considerations shape your position.

## Essay

Science and religion have shaped human civilization in different ways. Science seeks to explain the natural order of the world through observations and experiments, whereas religion provides ethical and spiritual values. Therefore, the claim that "Science is meaningless without religion" is too extreme. Science has great value on its own, but ethical values, including those prompted by religion, help ensure that scientific discoveries are used for the good of humanity.

One compelling reason is that science has significantly improved human life through innovations and discoveries. Advancements in technology, transportation, medicine, and communication have increased life expectancy and made human life easier. For example, the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) has developed affordable satellite technology that helps in weather forecasting, disaster management, and effective communication across India. Similarly, vaccines and modern medical research, such as Covaxin during COVID-19, were developed by Indian scientists and helped save millions of lives around the world. These achievements illustrate that science is meaningful because it solves real-world problems through evidence and experimentation.

At the same time, scientific knowledge can be misused if it is not guided by moral principles. Science can explain how to create powerful technologies, but it cannot show how to use them. The development of nuclear weapons during World War II is a lucid example of powerful and advanced scientific technology causing widespread destruction when ethical considerations were ignored. Likewise, modern fields such as genetic engineering and artificial intelligence raise significant questions about morality. Religious teachings, along with laws and human rights, can provide valuable guidance to ensure that scientific progress serves society responsibly.

Ultimately, science is not meaningless without religion because it aids in the betterment of human life by providing solutions to problems and using knowledge to make advancements. However, ethical values are important for guiding scientific progress toward peaceful and beneficial purposes. Rather than relying on either of them, science and religion can complement each other by combining knowledge and moral values, ultimately creating a more advanced and humane society.
