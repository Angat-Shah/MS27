## Essay

The claim contends that an action is morally correct if it produces more good than harm, while the reason states that the results of an action are more important than the intentions of the people performing it. Admittedly, the consequences of an action should play a significant role in judging its morality because actions that benefit society deserve recognition. However, determining whether an action is moral solely on the basis of its outcomes is an oversimplification. Intentions are equally important, especially in situations where the final result is influenced by factors beyond a person's control. Therefore, morality should be evaluated by considering both the consequences and the intentions behind an action.

There are many situations where positive outcomes justify calling an action morally correct. Governments and organizations often make difficult decisions with the goal of maximizing public welfare. For instance, during the COVID-19 pandemic, the Indian government imposed nationwide lockdowns that caused economic hardships for many people. Nevertheless, the primary objective was to reduce the spread of the virus and save millions of lives. Although the decision had negative consequences, many regarded it as justified because it sought to protect public health and produce a greater overall benefit for society. Similarly, large-scale vaccination campaigns may involve significant costs, but they prevent diseases and save countless lives. These examples support the claim that actions producing greater good than harm are generally considered morally acceptable.

However, judging morality only by the results can be unfair because outcomes are not always within human control. A doctor may perform a complex surgery with great skill and a sincere intention to save a patient's life, yet the patient may still die due to unforeseen complications. It would be unreasonable to judge the doctor's action as immoral simply because the outcome was negative. On the other hand, a pharmaceutical company may accidentally discover a medicine that benefits millions while conducting research primarily to increase its profits. Even though the result is positive, many people would still question whether the action was morally praiseworthy because the primary intention was financial gain rather than public welfare. These examples show that intentions remain an essential part of ethical judgment.

Taken together, the consequences of an action are an important measure of its moral value according to the claim, and society naturally appreciates actions that yield greater benefits than harm. However, this overlooks the limitation of the reason, which places the outcomes of an action above its intentions in every situation. Moral judgment should not ignore the motives behind an action, since people cannot always predict or control the results of their decisions. A balanced approach that considers both intentions and consequences provides a more accurate and fair standard for determining the morality of an action.
